CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED NINE
THE DAY AFTER

The day after Choosing Tide was deliberately empty. No formal lessons. No ceremonies until evening. No Order activities. Thorne had learned, according to Elowen, that newly placed first-years needed "a day to stop being perceived." Harper loved him for this. They slept until eight thirty. Ava called this sleeping late. Harper called it insulting. Breakfast was almost over when they arrived. For the first time, Jasper saw everyone wearing temporary Order trim in ordinary light. The effect was strange. People he had known as simply first-years now carried small visual markers of next year. Theo in Velmor edging. Soren Nocten. Freya Calyx. Mia Brannor. Nobody had changed overnight. Theo still stole one of Harper's pieces of toast. Good. Because they had promised.

Ava still corrected his version of what happened in the basin. Soren still had a book at breakfast. Freya still talked about weather. Orders had not sorted their personalities into boxes. After breakfast, Jasper, Harper and Ava went to West Courtyard. Harper arrived carrying three juice boxes.

"Saturday after breakfast."

Ava checked the courtyard clock.

"It is Friday."

Harper stopped. Jasper stared. They had made the promise weeks ago without checking the actual calendar.

"We are idiots," Harper said.

Ava looked offended.

"I am not."

"You made the plan."

Ava looked at the clock.

"We are collectively idiots."

Jasper laughed until he had to sit down. They decided the promise counted spiritually and agreed to return tomorrow anyway. The courtyard overlooked the sea but sat high enough that Water's presence, if there, remained quiet. They drank juice. Talked about nothing important. Then Harper said, "I thought Ava would get Nocten."

"We know," Ava replied.

"I thought Jasper could get Velmor."

Jasper nodded.

"I thought you were Brannor from the start."

Harper looked pleased. Ava said, "I thought Harper was Brannor, Jasper was uncertain and I was likely Nocten or Calyx."

"How does it feel being wrong?"

"Educational."

Harper grinned. Jasper looked at both scarves.

"Do you think we got Brannor because of each other?"

Ava considered.

"Not in a magical destiny sense."

"Obviously."

"But people affect one another. We spent a year making choices together. If the ritual responds partly to sustained choices, then our friendship may have shaped us."

Harper said, "So I made you cool."

"No."

"Scientific conclusion."

Jasper liked Ava's answer. They had not been assigned together because the universe needed a main trio. They might have become the kinds of people who fit the same Order partly because they had grown beside one another. That was more interesting. And less creepy. At midday they walked to the library. Soren sat in his usual place wearing Nocten trim. Jasper joined him. Harper went to find Mia. Ava disappeared into the archive section. Separate. Together. Both. Soren looked at Jasper's scarf.

"Brannor."

"Nocten."

"We have become enemies."

Jasper nodded solemnly.

"Obviously."

They lasted three seconds before laughing. Soren pulled out the tidal-lift book.

"I found another diagram."

They spent an hour discussing a machine that had not worked in seventy years. No Sea questions. Perfect. In the afternoon the first-years collected final reports. Jasper's was long enough to annoy him. Academics: solid. Practical magic: strongest in shield placement and controlled response. Ocean Studies: excellent observation, with note that unusual Sea sensitivity requires ongoing safety protocol and consent-based support. Social: strong peer relationships, should continue developing friendships beyond immediate residence group. Jasper smiled at that one. Soren had probably caused it. Personal development: increased tolerance of uncertainty; improving ability to distinguish responsibility from control. That sounded like Thorne. Harper's report contained the phrase "enthusiastic relationship with risk," which she considered insulting art.

Ava's said she should continue practicing decisions with incomplete information.

"They've all conspired," Ava said.

"Against your plans," Harper agreed.

At sunset, the first-years gathered for one final residence meeting. Elowen stood in the middle of the West common room.

"Tomorrow you become former first-years."

Theo said, "Promotion."

"Technically only after summer."

"Pre-promotion."

Elowen handed back the history boxes each room had stored. Jasper took Room Fourteen's.

"You will move these wherever you decide next year. Or take them home. Or throw them away."

Harper hugged the box.

"Never."

"As expected."

Elowen looked around at all of them.

"You arrived needing directions to breakfast. You now think you know the academy."

A few students smiled.

"You don't."

Laughter.

"That is the gift. Tidefall is larger than first year. So are you."

No long speech. She was learning too. Jasper carried the history box back to Room Fourteen. Three full days remained before departure. He expected sadness. Instead he felt full. Not finished. Full.

CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED TEN
THREE DAYS AS BRANNOR

The first thing Harper did on their first full day as Brannor was get lost in Brannor. Jasper considered this justice.

"The stairs all look the same."

Ava held the map.

"They do not."

"Stone. Rail. Door. More stone."

"This one has a red tile band."

"Tiny red tile band."

Priya waited at the next landing with her arms folded.

"You three finished?"

Harper pointed at the staircase.

"Bad architecture."

"It is two hundred years old."

"Time to improve."

The new Brannor students spent the morning learning their next-year spaces. Not moving in yet. Summer maintenance happened first. But their likely residence floor had been assigned. Jasper had expected rooms identical to first-year West. They were not. Brannor rooms were rectangular rather than round, built deeper inside the cliff with broad windows facing both sea and courtyard. Four beds each. Small desks. Shared shelves. A washroom between pairs of rooms. The biggest difference was the common area at the centre of the floor. Low couches. A long table. Noticeboard. A kitchenette students could use under year-level rules. Harper saw the kitchenette.

"Toast."

Ava said, "You are not allowed to use it alone until Year Three."

"Future toast."

Priya handed every new student a housing preference form. Jasper read it. ROOMMATE REQUESTS MAY BE CONSIDERED BUT ARE NOT GUARANTEED. Underneath:, Ava stared. STUDENTS MAY ALSO REQUEST NOT TO SHARE WITH A PARTICULAR PERSON. REASONS MAY BE GIVEN PRIVATELY TO STAFF. Jasper liked that second part more. Room assignments needed to work, not preserve appearances. Harper had already written JASPER + AVA in the preference box.

"You didn't ask."

Harper froze. Again. Then crossed it out.

"Right. Sorry."

She looked at them.

"Do you want to request same room?"

Jasper said yes. Ava said yes. Harper rewrote both names. Not a huge moment. Still mattered. Priya collected forms.

"You'll find out before next year. If not all three fit together, you will survive."

Harper said, "Debatable."

"We have counselors."

"Cruel."

They toured Brannor's study rooms. Workshop. Equipment store. Quiet room. That surprised Harper. A whole room with soft chairs, no games and a sign asking for silence.

"Brannor has quiet?"

Priya stared.

"We are not a species of drum."

Jasper nearly choked laughing. The quiet room overlooked the inland garden rather than the sea. He liked it immediately. Ava liked the small reference shelf. Harper lasted forty seconds before leaving. Then came responsibility boards. Every Brannor student took rotating community jobs. Common-room reset. Equipment check. Mentor table setup. Garden water. Emergency kit inspection with staff. No job was punishment. If someone repeatedly failed, staff worked out why before assigning consequences.

"What if someone just doesn't want to?" Harper asked.

Priya shrugged.

"Sometimes you do things because other people live with you."

Harper looked suspicious of society. At midday, Professor Keene joined the new students in the courtyard. He carried a crate.

"First Brannor job."

Harper brightened. Inside were damaged cushions. Her face fell.

"We repair these before summer storage."

Ava picked one up. Split seam. Simple. Students learned a basic hand stitch. No magic. Harper's first seam looked like an injured spider. Jasper's held. Ava's was so neat Keene checked whether she had secretly used a repair charm. She looked offended.

"I followed the instructions."

"Compliment."

"Oh."

Mia sat beside Harper and redid one corner.

"You are attacking fabric."

"It started it."

By lunch, ten cushions had been repaired. They ate on them outside. Jasper looked around. Brannor did not feel like winning. That was important. Choosing had not promoted them above Theo or Soren. It had given them a new set of people and expectations. At the end of the afternoon, Priya returned their chalk stones from the welcome feast.

"Take these home for summer. Bring them back next year."

"I managed."

"We're meant to bring them back."

"The box is coming back," Harper said.

Ava smiled.

"Fair."

Her stone joined theirs.

CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED ELEVEN

"No."
