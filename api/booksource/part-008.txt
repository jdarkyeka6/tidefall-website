d over.

"Go to sleep."

Harper whispered, "Traitor." The window did not break. Morning arrived grey and cold. The winter timetable included more indoor practical work and fewer long cliff sessions. Ocean Studies moved to a chart room overlooking the harbour, where first-years learned currents, coastal safety and weather signs. Professor Elowen began the first lesson after break with a rope.

"Who can tie a bowline?"

Nobody raised a hand. Harper said, "Can magic tie it?"

"Eventually. You first."

"Why?"

"Because if your spell fails on a wet dock, you still need the knot."

That answer ended the argument. They spent an hour producing terrible loops. Ava's first bowline was technically correct and tightened too small. Harper's was not a bowline but looked extremely secure. Jasper's fell apart when pulled. Soren somehow tied his around his own wrist. Professor Elowen walked the room repeating, "Again." By the end of class, Jasper could make one without looking at the instruction card. He liked that more than expected. The academy treated nonmagical skills with strange seriousness. Students learned first aid before healing spells. Knots before levitation on working platforms. Fire safety before heating charms. Maps before navigation magic.

"Magic gives you more ways to make mistakes," Ward told them. "It does not remove the old ones."

Harper wrote that sentence down. Ava looked shocked.

"You took notes."

"I take notes."

"You draw swords in the margins."

"Those are notes about swords."

Their ordinary magic also became more interesting. Foundations introduced layered control: starting one small effect, maintaining it, then performing another. Ava could hold a floating bead while moving a cloth almost immediately. Jasper managed after three lessons. Harper's bead repeatedly dropped whenever she focused on the cloth. She became furious. Then, remembering the water-heating lesson, she slowed down. By Friday she could hold both.

"Easy," she said.

"You practised every night," Jasper said.

"Lies."

Bag Bed contained two cracked practice beads as evidence. Winter also brought the first illnesses. Half the west residence developed coughs within a week. Mia lost her voice completely and communicated by writing rude signs. Soren kept reading despite a fever until Mae confiscated his book and sent him to the infirmary. Jasper avoided everything for six days, then woke with a blocked nose and the magical control of a distracted potato. His bead would not lift. Professor Elowen sent him back to residence.

"But I can do the lesson."

"You can barely breathe through your nose."

"That doesn't use my hands."

"Jasper."

He went. Harper and Ava returned after lunch carrying soup.

"We rescued this," Harper said.

"From the dining hall?"

"Very dramatic rescue."

Ava placed a stack of class notes on the desk. Jasper stared.

"Why?"

"You missed two lessons."

"I'm sick, not dead."

"Exactly."

Harper looked at the pages.

"I told her not to."

"You asked for a copy."

"For later."

Jasper drank soup and listened to them argue. The room felt warm. Outside, rain streaked the western windows. He realised, not for the first time, that friendship was mostly people becoming part of the background you missed when it was gone. The presence stayed quiet while he was ill. Jasper was almost offended. Then, on the first morning he returned to class, he crossed the western gallery and felt it brush his awareness. Cool. Brief. There. Jasper smiled despite himself. Ava caught him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

She raised an eyebrow.

"Sea."

"Strong?"

"No."

"Any image?"

"No."

Ava nodded and did not write it down. Progress.

CHAPTER TWENTY
THE SUPERVISED DOCK

The lower docks were less exciting when adults took you there legally. Harper considered this a design flaw.

"The rule made it sound dangerous."

Professor Elowen tightened the strap on a life jacket.

"It is dangerous if you ignore the rule."

"But we're not."

"Exactly."

Every first-year wore a bright orange life jacket. Harper hated hers. Ava checked the buckle twice. Jasper's sat too high against his chin and made turning his head annoying. The class descended through the same restricted corridor where the door had once opened by itself. The new silver ward plate recognised Professor Elowen's staff token. The lock clicked. This time the door opening felt ordinary. Jasper was grateful. A stone stair wound downward inside the cliff. Cold air rose from below. The sound of waves grew louder with each landing. At the bottom, the stair emerged into a covered platform above the academy's working harbour. The lower docks were not one dock. They were a system. Harper looked around.

A sheltered basin cut into the cliff, breakwater outside, two floating piers, one stone cargo platform and several narrow boat bays protected by heavy gates. Winches hung from beams. Ropes coiled on hooks. Warning lines marked wet edges. Older students in dark jackets worked with staff on one pier. A maintenance boat rocked near the outer wall.

"Okay. This is good."

Ava said, "The life jacket improved it?"

"No. Boats."

The lesson was marine safety. No mystery. Professor Elowen showed them where rescue rings were stored, how dock alarms worked, why students stood behind yellow loading lines, and what the tide gates did during storms.

"Year Four students who pass dock safety may use designated areas without direct staff supervision," she said. "That does not mean every area becomes open. Working zones remain working zones. Red closures remain closures."

Harper nodded solemnly. Ava whispered, "Remember this in three years."

"I remember everything."

"You forgot your left shoe last month."

"That was different."

Jasper barely heard them. The pressure had begun halfway down the stair. Here it was stronger than ever. Not painful. Dense. The presence filled the harbour without being located anywhere in it. Jasper could not point and say there. It felt beyond the breakwater. Below the platform. Far out at sea. All of those and none. Professor Elowen noticed his face.

"Jasper?"

He nodded.

"Strong."

She did not need the rest.

"Stay beside me."

Harper and Ava moved too. Elowen looked at them.

"You may stay."

No argument. The class continued to the outer observation rail. Five old metal markers were fixed into the stone below. Not the same as Ocean Studies tide markers. These were thick brackets set at uneven heights around the harbour wall. Several looked newer than the surrounding stone. Ava stared.

"Safety boundaries?"

Professor Elowen followed her gaze.

"Part of them."

"Deepwater?"

Elowen was quiet.

"Some were replaced after Deepwater."

Harper pointed to one high bracket.

"Why there?"

"Old surge line."

Jasper's chest tightened. A flash hit him. Water. Not the harbour as it was now. Dark water rising against the stone. A person kneeling near an edge. Another shape running toward them. Then confusion. Fear. A huge pressure in the sea. The image vanished. Jasper grabbed the rail. Professor Elowen caught his shoulder.

"Sit."

He sat immediately on a bench behind the safety line. Harper crouched in front of him.

"What?"

Jasper told them. A person kneeling. Someone running. Surge. Nothing clear enough to identify faces. Ava looked at the old brackets.

"Deepwater memory?"

Professor Elowen did not answer immediately.

"Possibly."

Jasper looked at her.

"Can it remember?"

"If what you're sensing is a conscious magical presence, memory would not be impossible."

Harper said, "That was a very careful sentence."

"Yes."

Ava asked, "Do you think it is conscious?" Elowen looked out through the breakwater.

"I think some events in the records are difficult to explain without choice. That is not proof of what kind of thing is choosing."

Jasper's skin prickled.

"So you think something's there."

"I think pretending there is nothing would be irresponsible."

That was the closest any adult had come. Professor Elowen looked at him.

"How do you feel now?"

"Fine."

"Pressure?"

"Still there."

"Stronger?"

"No."

"Then we stay five minutes. If it changes, we leave."

Jasper nodded. The class moved toward the inner pier. He stayed with Elowen, Harper and Ava. The pressure remained. Not pushing. Not demanding. Aware. Jasper looked at the old Deepwater brackets. The presence sent no more images. After five minutes, it faded. Professor Elowen let them rejoin the class. Harper finally got to step onto a floating pier. She looked delighted until it moved under her feet.

"I don't like that."

Ava smiled.

"Adventure."

"Shut up."

They practised throwing rescue ropes. Jasper missed the target three times. Harper hit it but threw so much rope that half the coil landed in the water. Ava landed closest. By the end of the session, the lower docks felt less like a forbidden magical location and more like a place where people worked. That mattered. Deepwater had happened here. So had thousands of ordinary mornings. The presence had once been involved in something dangerous. It did not make every rope, boat and wave part of the mystery. As they climbed back toward the academy, Harper said, "Still going there first day of Year Four." Ava said, "During open hours."

"Yes."

"After safety training."

"Yes."

"Not at night."

Harper sighed.

"Why are you ruining future me's fun?"

Jasper laughed. Behind them, the lower door locked normally.

CHAPTER TWENTY-ONE
THE WRONG KIND OF BRAVE

The first-year courage exercise was not supposed to be about courage. Professor Ward said so four times. That guaranteed everyone called it the courage exercise. The indoor training hall had been rearranged with low obstacles, padded walls and narrow balance beams no more than half a metre from the floor. Students worked in pairs. One crossed while the other used simple protective magic to move soft obstacles out of the way.

"This tests communication," Ward said.

Harper whispered, "Courage." Ava whispered back, "You are the problem." Jasper was paired with Mia for the first run. Mia crossed the beam while Jasper redirected hanging pads.

"Left," she said.

Jasper moved the left pad.

"Other left."

"That is your left!"

"I'm facing you!"

The pad bumped her shoulder. She laughed and nearly stepped off.

"Sorry."

"Pay attention."

"You said left."

They finished without disaster. Harper was paired with Ava. This became an event. Harper stepped onto the beam. Ava stood beside the obstacle controls.

"Wait for me to clear each one."

"Yep."

Harper reached the first pad. Ava moved it. Second. Moved. Third. Harper jumped over it before Ava acted. Ward blew a whistle.

"Reset."

Harper stopped.

"I cleared it."

"You ignored your partner."

"I didn't need—"

Ward waited. Harper closed her mouth. Reset. Again. This time Harper waited. At the final obstacle, Ava's spell slipped. The hanging pad swung back into the path. Harper could have jumped. Jasper saw her body prepare. Then she stopped. Ava corrected. Pad moved. Harper crossed. Ward nodded.

"Good."

Harper stepped down.

"That was slower."

"Yes."

"I hate this class."

"No you don't."

Harper thought.

"No."

Afterward, a Year Three student named Cal was showing off near the practice rail outside the hall. The rail guarded a shallow stairwell, not a cliff. Still, he had climbed onto the lower bar and was balancing with both arms out. Harper stopped.

"That's stupid."

Jasper looked at her.

"You said jumping over moving pads was courage ten minutes ago."

"That had a mat."

Cal took another step. A prefect shouted at him to get down. He did, laughing. Harper watched.

"I used to think if you're scared and do it anyway, that's brave."

Ava said, "That can be brave."

"Yeah, but sometimes you're scared because your brain is correctly saying don't do that."

Jasper smiled.

"Professor Ward got you."

"No."

"She did."

"Absolutely not."

That evening a storm shut the outer courtyards. The trio sat in Room Fourteen with homework. Harper was supposed to write four sentences about communication in protective magic. She had written one: WAITING IS ANNOYING BUT SOMETIMES IMPORTANT. Ava read it.

"Technically correct."

"Done."

"Four sentences."

Harper added: AVA LIKES WAITING TOO MUCH.

"That is not relevant."

"It is personal reflection."

Jasper tried to work while laughing. Later, after lights-out, wind pushed hard against the western window. Jasper woke sometime after midnight. The room was dark. A faint pressure sat in his chest. He stayed in bed. He did not reach. He did not test. The pressure grew slightly. Jasper thought of Deepwater. A student convinced response meant control. He thought of Harper stopping on the beam even though she could have jumped. Waiting was annoying but sometimes important. Jasper breathed. The presence remained for several minutes. Then faded. Nothing happened. He went back to sleep. In the morning, he told Ava and Harper. Harper looked impressed.

"You didn't go to the window?"

"No."

"Good."

Ava nodded. Jasper looked between them.

"Why do I feel like I passed a test nobody gave me?"

Harper said, "Because we are proud of you." Then made a disgusted face.

"Don't make me say that again."

Jasper laughed.

CHAPTER TWENTY-TWO
THE OLD MAINTENANCE PASSAGE

They broke a rule by accident. At least it began by accident. The west residence heating stopped working during one particularly cold July morning. Mae sent everyone to breakfast wearing jumpers and said maintenance would fix it before lunch. By lunch, the room was still cold. By dinner, Harper had turned Bag Bed into a blanket fortress.

"We live here now."

Ava stood outside it.

"We already lived here."

"Different government."

Jasper crawled inside because it was warmer. Ava lasted four minutes before joining them. At eight o'clock, the heating pipes in the western wall made a loud bang. Then another. A small panel beside the wardrobe popped open. Everyone stared. Behind it was darkness. Harper emerged from the blanket fortress immediately.

"Absolutely not," Ava said.

"I haven't moved."

"Your face moved."

Jasper approached the panel. A cold draught came through. Not sea air. Dust and stone. He shone his bedside lamp inside. A narrow maintenance space ran behind the wall, just wide enough for pipes and a person to crouch.

"Heating access," Ava said.

Harper said, "Tunnel."

"It is a service cavity."

"Tunnel."

Jasper found a metal label on the inside edge. WEST RESIDENCE HEAT LOOP - ACCESS STAFF ONLY

"Staff only," he said.

Harper sighed.

"Everything interesting is staff only."

A pipe banged again. Warm air puffed from somewhere inside. Then the light at the end of the cavity flickered. Not Jasper's lamp. Farther down. A faint blue glow. The trio went quiet.

"Maintenance worker?" Jasper whispered.

Ava said, "Could be." Harper called, "Hello?" No answer. The glow moved. Jasper's chest tightened. Sea pressure. Here? Inside the wall? That made no sense. He crouched closer.

"I feel it."

Ava's expression changed.

"Then close the panel."

Harper looked surprised.

"Really?"

"Yes. Staff only plus unknown light plus Jasper's sea thing. That's three reasons."

Harper thought.

"Fair."

Jasper reached for the panel. The blue glow brightened. A sound came from inside. Metal scraping stone. Then something small rolled out of the darkness and stopped near Jasper's hand. A brass disc. Old. Green around the edges. Stamped with a wave symbol. Ava stared.

"Do not touch it."

Jasper was already not touching it. Harper said, "Why?"

"Because it came from a restricted tunnel by itself."

"Strong argument."

They called Mae. Mae called maintenance. Maintenance called Professor Crowe. Within twenty minutes Room Fourteen had four adults in it and no children allowed near the wardrobe. Professor Crowe wore gloves and lifted the brass disc into a small box.

"Where was it?"

Jasper explained.

"And you felt the marine sensation?"

"Yeah."

Crowe looked into the service passage.

"Interesting."

Harper said, "What is it?" Crowe held up the box.

"Old ward token."

Ava leaned forward.

"For what ward?"

"That is what I intend to find out."

"Deepwater?"

Crowe's eyebrows rose.

"You three have been reading."

Harper said, "The goat has more records." Crowe looked confused. Ava said, "Library history."

"Right."

The adults opened the service passage properly. The blue light came from a maintenance charm farther inside. Boring. The token had likely been knocked loose when pipes shifted. Also boring. Jasper's pressure remained unexplained. Not boring. They were sent to the common room while repairs continued. Harper dropped onto a sofa.

"We did everything right."

Ava nodded.

"We did."

"And still found a secret tunnel."

"Service cavity."

"Secret tunnel."

Jasper looked toward the closed residence door.

"Why did I feel it?"

Ava pulled out her notebook.

"Maybe the token."

"How?"

"Old marine ward. If it was designed after Deepwater—"

"We don't know it was."

"Correct."

Harper groaned.

"I hate when both of you become sensible at the same time."

The heating was repaired before bedtime. Professor Crowe returned the next morning. She did not return the brass disc.

"It predates Deepwater," she said. "Old harbour ward token. Probably part of a system retired decades ago."

Ava asked, "Why did Jasper feel the sea when it moved?" Crowe looked at him.

"I don't know."

Jasper nodded. The answer no longer surprised him. Crowe continued.

"But the token was designed to register large changes in water pressure near the cliffs. It may hold residual magic from repeated use."

Jasper thought about memory. Not only living things remembered. Magic could leave traces too. That made the world more complicated. It also gave him something useful. Not every strange feeling meant the presence was actively speaking to him. Sometimes old magic could echo. Ava added a new heading to her notebook: PRESENCE vs RESIDUE? Jasper approved. Harper drew a tiny tunnel beside it
