CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED SEVENTEEN
SUMMER MAGIC

Tidefall sent every first-year home with summer magic rules.

Harper hated them by post.

Jasper knew because her first letter arrived with SUMMER TYRANNY written across the envelope.

Inside, she had written:

JASPER,

Mum has read the rules three times and now every time I touch anything she asks if it is "within supervised first-year scope." I made my bed with movement magic and she got the handbook.

This is somehow your fault.

P.S. Brannor forever.

P.P.S. My rock is on my desk.

P.P.P.S. Ava sent me a letter with headings.

P.P.P.P.S. DO NOT TELL AVA I COMPLAINED ABOUT THE HEADINGS.

Jasper laughed loudly enough that his mother looked over from the kitchen.

"Harper?"

"How did you know?"

"You only laugh like you're being attacked when it's Harper."

Jasper wrote back.

He did not tell Ava about the headings.

He did tell Ava that Harper had specifically instructed him not to tell her about something.

This caused three more letters.

The summer rules were not actually terrible.

First-years could practise effects already signed off as safe, with a guardian aware of what they were doing and within strict limits.

Small-object movement.

Basic Blinka-lights.

Controlled warming.

Simple drying and mending.

No new spells from books.

No magical competitions in public.

No testing shields against anything harder than approved soft practice objects.

No deciding that "I almost know how" counted as permission.

Jasper's mother watched every home practice session as though she expected the kitchen table to explode.

He floated three bottle caps across it.

One fell.

His father clapped.

"Do not encourage mediocrity," Jasper said.

"I support my son."

"It fell."

"Dramatically."

Jasper practised shield shaping with rolled socks.

His mother threw them gently.

He blocked five.

Missed two.

His father walked through at exactly the wrong moment and received a sock to the shoulder.

"I have entered combat."

"Sorry."

"I surrender."

They stopped.

That was the strange thing about summer magic.

Nobody was trying to make him improve quickly.

There was no next assessment.

No class chart.

No reason to use Essence simply because it was available.

Jasper could stand up to get a book.

He could walk three metres to switch on a lamp.

He could carry a cup with his hand.

At Tidefall, magic lived inside lessons and systems.

At home, it became optional.

Jasper liked that.

Thorne's final instruction returned to him.

Learn something useless.

So Jasper learned card tricks.

Not magical card tricks.

Real ones.

His father found an old beginner book and showed him how to hold the deck without announcing to everyone that he was hiding something.

Jasper's first attempt was terrible.

His mother saw the card immediately.

"Again."

By the end of the week, Jasper could make one appear from behind his father's ear badly enough that everybody still laughed.

It was useless.

Completely.

Jasper sent Thorne a postcard through Tidefall's summer office.

LEARNING USELESS CARD TRICKS. YOUR FAULT.

Two weeks later, a reply arrived.

GOOD.

Nothing else.

Jasper put the postcard in the history box.

Summer filled itself with ordinary things.

Family visits.

Shops.

Books.

Letters from Harper.

More organised letters from Ava.

Magic practice every few days.

No Water Titan.

At first Jasper counted the silence.

One day.

Three days.

A week.

Then he stopped.

Maybe Titan-sense worked best at Tidefall.

Maybe the Water Titan was simply elsewhere.

Maybe nothing needed to happen.

He did not turn summer into an experiment.

When Jasper missed Tidefall, he discovered he missed the people more than the mystery.

That felt important.

CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED EIGHTEEN
THE ROOM LETTER

The Brannor room assignment arrived in mid-summer. Jasper saw the Tidefall crest on the envelope and shouted for his mother before opening it. She came running fast enough to think something was wrong.

"What?"

"Room."

She stopped.

"You shouted like the house was on fire."

"Sorry."

"Open it."

Jasper tore the top carefully anyway. BRANNOR YEAR TWO RESIDENCE ASSIGNMENT. Room B-12. Capacity: four students. Assigned students: JASPER HOLLOWAY HARPER VALE AVA REES, Fourth bed: UNASSIGNED. Jasper read the last line twice. His mother read over his shoulder.

"So all three of you are together."

"Yeah."

Relief arrived so quickly it almost embarrassed him. The fourth bed being empty did not. Room Fourteen had taught him that a four-bed room did not need four names to be a proper room. Tidefall used flexible capacity. Some rooms filled. Some did not. Students arrived at different times, moved for practical reasons, or simply left spaces unused. Infrastructure, as Ava would say. Not prophecy. Jasper immediately wrote two letters. Harper's reply arrived first despite living farther from the mail exchange. B-12. ALL THREE. BRANNOR HAS EXCELLENT ADMINISTRATION. ALSO I CALL THE BED NEAREST THE WINDOW. Ava's arrived later:, Jasper wrote back:, Jasper replied:

I am pleased. Please note Brannor did not "win" anything. Also B-12 is two corridors from the common room and one floor above the quiet room. I checked the map. The unassigned bed should remain unassigned unless residence staff place someone there. Do not let Harper turn it into Bag Bed Two before term starts. Of course she had checked the map. I cannot promise anything about Bag Bed Two. A postcard from Mia arrived the following week. She had been assigned a room nearby with two other Brannor students and one open place. PLEASE INFORM HARPER THAT BEING IN THE SAME ORDER DOES NOT GIVE HER PERMISSION TO STEAL MY HAND BALL. Tell her yourself. This is self-protection. Fewer first-year items. More subject books. Bring Brannor stone.

The B-12 letter included a supply list. Order scarf with permanent trim would be ready on return. Bring one personal item for room shelf if desired. Jasper chose the tiny counterweight lift Soren had made. The history box would also return, but not as a room decoration unless Harper and Ava agreed. Consent rules even for sentimental junk. His mother liked the new room arrangement until she saw "upper Brannor floor."

"How high?"

"Not cliff-high."

"That is not a measurement."

"Ava says one floor above the quiet room."

"I don't know where that is."

"Neither do I exactly."

She stared. Jasper smiled.

"There are railings."

"That sentence has not improved in a year."

He pinned the room letter above his desk. Three names. Four beds. An empty place with no special meaning at all. Next year would fill it with books, damp towels, arguments, homework and whatever Harper refused to put away. That was enough.

CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED NINETEEN
ORDINARY OCEAN

Near the end of summer, Jasper's family spent a Sunday at an ordinary beach near home.

Not Tidefall's beach.

No hidden road, no cliff wards and no Academy towers above the water. There were families beneath umbrellas, children digging enormous holes for reasons known only to children, people carrying boards toward the surf and the mixed smell of sunscreen, salt and hot chips.

Jasper walked along the edge with water around his ankles.

His parents had settled farther up the sand, close enough that he could hear his father complaining about gulls.

For months, Jasper had not felt the Water Titan.

He had expected that silence to bother him more.

The ocean was bright and noisy today, full of ordinary waves collapsing over themselves. Jasper watched the horizon and let the questions come without doing anything with them.

Was the Water Titan only close to Tidefall?

Could something connected to water be nearer in one place than another?

Did Jasper's Titan-sense depend on the Academy cliffs, the old Selari magic beneath them or something else entirely?

Had the first dream come from Water, or simply from Jasper being frightened about leaving for school?

He did not know.

A year earlier, that list would have been unbearable.

Now it was just a list.

A wave curled around his ankles and retreated.

Nothing pressed behind his ribs.

Jasper picked up a shell, looked at it and put it back where he found it.

No investigation required.

His father called from the towels.

"Chips!"

Jasper turned immediately.

Some signals deserved urgent action.

They ate beneath the umbrella while a gull watched from a carefully calculated stealing distance.

His mother asked, "Excited for Year Two?"

"Yeah."

"Nervous?"

"Yeah."

"Good."

Jasper looked at her. "Why good?"

"Because two answers can be true at once."

He narrowed his eyes.

"Ava has infected you."

"I like Ava."

"Everyone likes Ava."

"Does Harper?"

"Especially Harper. She just complains louder."

Jasper's father stole one of his chips.

Jasper stole two back.

The gull took this as evidence that civilisation was collapsing and moved closer.

"Not today," Dad told it.

The gull remained unconvinced.

On the drive home, Jasper fell asleep against the window.

There was no water dream.

When he woke, the coast had disappeared behind suburbs and roads.

That evening he found the temporary Brannor scarf from Choosing Tide folded on his chair. His mother had washed it carefully. Permanent uniform trim would replace it next year, but Jasper wanted to keep the first one.

He folded it again and carried it to the history box.

The sea had been ordinary all day.

Jasper realised he was glad.

CHAPTER ONE HUNDRED TWENTY
THE FIRST YEAR

The night before the history box went into the hall cupboard for summer, Jasper opened it one more time. His bedroom was quiet. There were no Tidefall bells sounding through stone and no waves striking the Academy cliffs far below a western window. Outside were suburban roofs, a streetlight, the distant noise of a car and a magpie that had apparently decided night was an acceptable time to complain. The box smelled faintly of paper, dust and salt. Jasper sat on the floor and lifted the lid. He did not take everything out. There was too much now, and that felt like the point.

The blue cloth from his first Foundations lesson was folded beneath the west-stair pebble. Harper's race ribbon had wrapped itself around a safety card. Ava's first measuring sheet sat under the Chronicle beside a Brannor stone and the little yellow mechanical bookmark Jasper had forgotten he owned until that moment. He smiled at the cloth. At the beginning of the year he had spent several minutes trying to move something lighter than a handkerchief by one centimetre.

Now he could hold a shield, warm water without boiling it and use enough Essence to move several things at once if Professor Elowen was nearby to tell him when he was getting sloppy. That felt like a better kind of progress than anything the sea had done. Jasper opened the Chronicle. A page near the back still carried the heading Ava had written months earlier:

THINGS YOU ARE ALLOWED NOT TO KNOW YET.

There were more things on the list now than there had been when she started it. What exactly was Titan-sense? Why could Jasper reach the Water Titan when Alaric Thorne seemed able to hear only echoes? Why had the water moved for Harper when she fell? Why had nothing answered when Benji fell? What had happened during the strange pause in Jasper's Choosing? How much of Tidefall's oldest Selari history had been misunderstood because nobody alive had the whole story? At the beginning of first year, six unanswered questions would have driven Jasper mad. Now they mostly looked like next year's problem. He found a pencil and added one sentence at the bottom.

You are allowed to leave some questions for later. Jasper read it twice. Then he shut the Chronicle. Some things from the year were not inside the box at all. Harper talking after lights-out when everyone else wanted to sleep. Ava labelling socks because being organised was easier than admitting she was nervous. Bad toast. Handball arguments. Ancient Selari that turned out to contain complaints about wet stairs as well as warnings about Titans. The night market. The storm. Harper falling. Benji falling. Those two memories would probably never fit together neatly.

The Water Titan had moved once and not the other time. Jasper had spent months wanting a rule that explained why. He had imagined that if he found the rule, he could work out what he should have done differently. There might not be one. That did not mean Benji's fall was Jasper's fault. It did not mean Harper's rescue belonged to him either. Hearing something was not the same as controlling it. Jasper put the Chronicle back. His temporary Brannor scarf went on top. Grey cloth, gold edge, slightly crooked where it had been folded after Choosing Tide. Next year he would return to Tidefall as a Brannor student.

Harper would almost certainly claim the spare bed in B-12 for bags before anyone could stop her. Ava would object and then create a storage system. Soren would vanish into Nocten's library spaces. Mia would demand another handball match. Lessons would get harder. The fourth bed would still be empty. Jasper did not know who would eventually sleep there. He did not need to. His father knocked on the open door.

"Finished?"

"Nearly."

Dad looked at the box. "Still weighs approximately the same as a small car?"

"Harper gave me rocks."

"Of course she did."

Jasper closed the lid. His father carried the box into the hall while Jasper switched off the bedroom lamp. Darkness settled around the room. For a moment he listened automatically. No pressure touched his chest. No impossible awareness turned toward him from far beyond Perth. There was only the ordinary night. Months ago, silence had felt like proof that something was missing. Now Jasper lay back against his pillow and understood that silence could simply mean nothing was asking for his attention. That was fine. Tidefall would still be there next year. So would the sea. Jasper closed his eyes. This time, he did not wait for an answer.
