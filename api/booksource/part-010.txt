A thing that could choose was something else.

CHAPTER TWENTY-SEVEN
THE INFIRMARY WINDOW

Harper had a bruised hip, a cut on one elbow, two scraped palms and an extremely poor attitude about staying in bed. This was the official medical assessment according to Harper. The healer's version included several more words.

"Nothing broken," Healer Moss said for the third time. "No head injury. You swallowed some seawater and your body has had a shock. You are staying here tonight."

"I feel fine."

"You fell off a cliff."

"Technically I fell off a ledge."

Healer Moss looked at Professor Elowen.

"Is she always like this?"

Elowen said, "Increasingly." Jasper hated that thought. Harper saw him staring. Jasper and Ava stood just inside the infirmary door. They had been allowed in after nearly an hour of waiting. Harper sat propped against white pillows wearing an infirmary shirt too large for her. Her hair was still damp at the ends. A bandage covered one elbow. She looked smaller than usual.

"Stop."

"What?"

"That face."

"I don't have a face."

"You absolutely have a face."

Ava put the bag of Harper's dry clothes on a chair.

"He means you nearly died."

Harper stared at her.

"Excellent comforting."

Ava's eyes filled. The joke died. Harper's expression changed immediately.

"Hey."

Ava looked away.

"I'm okay."

"I know."

"No, like actually. Look."

Harper lifted both hands.

"All fingers."

Ava laughed despite the tears. Jasper sat on the chair beside the bed. He had not cried yet. That worried him. He felt like he should. Instead everything inside him was packed too tightly. Harper looked at him.

"You too."

"What?"

"Say something."

Jasper opened his mouth. Nothing useful appeared.

"It caught you."

Harper went still. The infirmary sounds seemed louder. Rain beginning against the high windows. A trolley rolling in another room. Healer Moss speaking quietly with Professor Elowen near the doorway. Harper looked down at her hands.

"Yeah."

Ava sat on the end of the bed.

"The swell formed directly beneath your fall. Wind direction did not support it. The timing—"

Harper said, "Ava." Ava stopped.

"Sorry."

Harper rubbed one bandaged palm.

"I know."

Jasper looked at her.

"You felt it?"

"I felt water."

"No. Like... anything else?"

Harper shook her head.

"I was busy falling."

That made sense.

"I felt it," Jasper said.

Neither girl spoke.

"More than before. It wasn't just watching. It... decided."

Harper frowned.

"Decided what?"

"To catch you."

The word catch was too simple. The wave had not formed arms. Harper had still hit water hard enough to cough for twenty minutes. But without it, she would have fallen much farther. They all knew that. Ava asked, "Did it react to you?" Jasper thought carefully.

"No."

"You didn't reach?"

"I didn't have time."

He replayed the instant. Harper going over. Pressure exploding into awareness. The swell rising.

"It was already moving."

Ava's face changed.

"Independent response."

Jasper nodded. Harper looked between them.

"English."

Ava said, "It didn't wait for Jasper." Harper looked toward the infirmary window. The sea was visible as a strip of dark grey beyond the courtyard roofs.

"So why me?"

No one answered. That was the worst question in the room. Jasper had no idea. Ava had no pattern. Harper did not look grateful anymore. She looked angry.

"I don't like that."

Jasper understood.

"Me neither."

"I don't want it choosing whether I get to live."

Healer Moss looked over at that. Professor Elowen came closer.

"You are allowed to be angry."

Harper looked at her.

"Was it the sea?"

Elowen paused.

"The water moved in a way I cannot explain naturally."

"That's not what I asked."

"I know."

Harper's jaw tightened. Elowen sat on the edge of the second chair.

"I believe something acted. I don't know what name to give it."

Harper looked back at the window.

"Did it save me?"

"It changed the fall. Staff still reached you. The lower platform still mattered. Your own body still survived the impact. We do not need to turn one impossible thing into the only reason you are here."

That helped. Jasper saw it. Harper breathed out slowly.

"Okay."

Professor Elowen looked at all three.

"Headmaster Thorne will speak with you tomorrow. Tonight, nobody solves anything."

Harper said, "I'm in a bed."

"Exactly."

Jasper and Ava stayed until visiting hour ended. Before leaving, Jasper put the west-stair pebble from Bag Bed on Harper's bedside table. She stared.

"Why did you bring that?"

"You nearly fell there too."

"I know."

"Thought you'd want it."

Harper picked it up.

"This is a terrible gift."

"Give it back."

She closed her fist around it.

"No."

Back in Room Fourteen, the empty space where Harper should have been felt huge. Her bed was unmade. Her shoes sat crooked near the door. One sleeve hung from a drawer. Ava changed into pyjamas without speaking. Jasper sat on his own bed.

"Ava?"

"Yeah?"

"You okay?"

She nodded. Then shook her head. Jasper moved to the desk chair. Neither knew what to say. After a while Ava opened the Map of Things That Should Not Happen. Jasper expected notes. Instead she turned to the page about Harper's fall and drew one line through the entire section.

"What are you doing?"

"Not tonight."

She closed the book. Jasper smiled slightly. They went to bed. The sea was loud beyond the windows. Jasper felt no presence. For the first time, he was glad it stayed away.

CHAPTER TWENTY-EIGHT
EVERYONE KNOWS

By breakfast, everyone knew Harper had fallen. By lunch, everyone knew the sea had caught her. By dinner, Harper had apparently fallen from the highest tower during a lightning storm and been lifted back to the academy by a twenty-metre wave. Harper had not even returned from the infirmary yet. Rumours were efficient. Mia sat beside Jasper at lunch.

"Someone in North residence says you made it happen."

Jasper stopped eating.

"Made what happen?"

"The wave."

Ava looked up sharply.

"On what evidence?"

Mia shrugged.

"Because you were there when the Ocean Studies weird thing happened too."

Jasper's stomach turned. There it was. The exact mistake. Response means control. He pushed his plate away.

"I didn't."

"I know."

"How?"

Mia looked confused.

"Because you looked more scared than anyone."

That was not evidence either. But Jasper appreciated it. The school made an announcement that afternoon. Not about magic. About safety. The north observation ledge closed while staff inspected the old barrier posts and drainage channel. All first-year coastal classes moved to the main terrace. Professor Crowe explained that winter water had loosened grit under part of the painted boundary and that the lower gap between two old posts should have been covered.

"The academy failed to account for a hazard," she said during first-year assembly. "We are correcting it."

Jasper noticed that wording. Not Harper failed. Not freak accident. The academy. Harper returned after dinner. The west residence greeted her like she had won a war. She hated it.

"Stop clapping."

Nobody stopped. Mia hugged her. Soren handed her a book because apparently that was how he handled emotional situations. Theo asked whether she remembered the fall. Mae told him not to ask. Harper said, "I remember enough." Jasper and Ava followed. Harper shut the door. Then escaped to Room Fourteen.

"I hate everyone."

Ava said, "You liked Mia."

"I hate almost everyone."

She sat on her bed. The west-stair pebble remained in her pocket. Jasper noticed but did not mention it.

"People think you made the wave," Harper said.

He looked down.

"Mia told me."

"They're idiots."

Ava said, "They are drawing a simple causal link from repeated correlation." Harper looked at her.

"Idiots."

"Simplified, yes."

Jasper sat at the desk.

"What if teachers think that too?"

Ava said, "Elowen doesn't."

"Thorne won't."

Harper's certainty helped. Then she added, "If he does, I'll tell him he's wrong."

"Please don't threaten the Headmaster."

"I didn't say threaten."

The next morning, Thorne held a meeting in his study. This time Professor Elowen and Crowe were there too. Harper's parents joined by a secure communication mirror in the wall, which was the first genuinely impressive long-distance magic Harper had seen. She spent thirty seconds being amazed before remembering the situation. Her mother looked like she had not slept. Her father looked worse. Thorne explained the physical investigation first. Loose grit. Drainage. Barrier gap. Repairs. Then the water.

"We have instrument records from the harbour," Crowe said. "The swell beneath the ledge was not consistent with wind or background tide."

Harper's mum closed her eyes briefly.

"So something magical happened."

"Yes."

Thorne did not hide behind maybe. Jasper appreciated that.

"Was my daughter targeted?"

Thorne's face tightened.

"We have no evidence of an attack."

Harper said, "It helped." Her parents looked at her.

"I don't like it," she added quickly. "But it did."

Jasper spoke before anyone asked him.

"I felt the presence before the wave moved. It wasn't reacting to me."

Harper's dad looked at him through the mirror.

"How do you know?"

Jasper thought carefully.

"Because I didn't do anything. It was already there."

Ava added, "The wave formed before Jasper reached the wall." Crowe nodded.

"Witness statements support that."

Thorne looked at Jasper.

"This matters."

Jasper knew.

"Communication is not command."

"Yes."

Harper's mum leaned toward the mirror.

"Harper, do you want to come home?"

Harper froze. Nobody had asked that yet.

"Now?"

"For a while. Or for the term. Whatever you need."

Harper looked around the study. At Jasper. Ava. Elowen. Then at her parents.

"No."

Her mum breathed out.

"Are you sure?"

"I want to stay."

"That can change."

"I know."

Her father said, "No hero points for staying if you want home." Harper smiled faintly.

"I know."

The meeting ended with more safety rules and no explanation of why the presence chose Harper. Outside, students stared as the trio crossed Founders' Courtyard. A younger first-year whispered, "That's her." Harper stopped. Jasper expected anger. Instead she turned.

"Hi."

The girl nearly jumped.

"Hi."

"I fell. I'm fine. The teachers fixed the ledge. That's all."

The girl nodded rapidly. Harper kept walking. Jasper caught up.

"That's not all."

"I know."

"Then why say it?"

Harper shoved her hands into her pockets.

"Because I don't want to be the girl the ocean saved."

Ava walked on her other side.

"You aren't."

Harper looked at her. Ava continued.

"You are Harper. That happened to you. It isn't your name."

Harper was quiet. Then said, "That was actually good." Ava looked pleased.

"Thank you."

"Don't get used to it."

Jasper smiled. The rumours lasted another week. Then someone in Year Two accidentally turned a practice dummy bright purple and school attention moved on. Children were efficient that way too.

CHAPTER TWENTY-NINE
THE CHOICE IN THE WATER

Jasper asked Alaric Thorne one question after their meeting.

"Why Harper?"

Thorne did not know.

That should have been the end of it, but the question followed Jasper through the next week like a pebble stuck in his shoe.

Why Harper?

Students had slipped at the tide pools before. Waves had knocked people off balance. Harper herself had tripped often enough that Ava had once suggested keeping a separate count. None of those moments had made the sea rise beneath somebody as though it had been waiting for exactly that fall.

Ava made a list of possibilities because that was what Ava did when the world refused to explain itself.

She read them aloud in Room Fourteen while Harper lay upside down on Bag Bed.

"One: location. The north observation ledge may sit above a particular current or old magical channel. Two: timing. The presence may already have been active for a completely different reason. Three: Jasper. His being there may have mattered even if he did not cause anything. Four: Harper."

Harper sat upright. "Delete four."

"It is a possibility."

"I do not have sea powers."

"I did not write sea powers."

"You were thinking sea powers."

"I was thinking unknown variable."

"That is worse."

Ava left theory four where it was and added a fifth line.

UNKNOWN.

Jasper liked that one best.

Ocean Studies changed for several weeks after the fall. Professor Elowen moved first-years away from the outer ledges and into enclosed observation rooms, where they worked with tide charts, model channels and long glass wave tanks. Some students complained about losing the view.

Harper did not.

That was how Jasper knew the fall had frightened her more than she wanted to say.

One afternoon Elowen placed a stone block in the middle of the wave tank and asked what would happen when the current reached it.

Ava's hand went up. "The flow splits around the block, pressure rises on the upstream side, and turbulence forms behind it."

Harper raised hers after Ava had finished. "Water goes around."

Elowen nodded. "Also correct."

The class laughed, including Ava.

Jasper watched the little current divide around the stone. The water itself did not decide anything. It responded to shape, pressure and movement.

The thought stayed with him.

After class, he found Thorne in the western gallery with Professor Crowe. Jasper had not been looking for either of them, and for a moment he considered turning around.

Thorne saw him first.

"Jasper?"

Crowe collected her folder and left without making the moment feel like an appointment.

Jasper stood beside the open window. Far below, ordinary waves struck the cliff.

"I think I've been saying it wrong," he said.

Thorne waited.

"When Harper fell, I keep saying the sea chose to move. But water doesn't choose. If something chose, the water was just what it used."

Thorne's expression changed by a fraction.

"That is possible."

"You already thought that."

"I had considered it."

Jasper frowned. "Why didn't you tell me?"

Thorne leaned one shoulder against the stone window frame. "Because when an adult gives a seven-year-old a theory, it can become a fact very quickly. If I tell you there is a creature under the sea, you may begin making every strange feeling fit that story. If I tell you there is only old environmental magic, you may do exactly the same thing in the other direction."

"So you just say maybe."

"Often."

"That's annoying."

"Usually."

Jasper looked back at the water.

"Do you think something is thinking down there?"

Thorne was quiet for several seconds. "After Harper's fall, I think that is more possible than I did before."

That was not an answer, but it was more than Jasper had expected.

"Could it have saved her because I was there?"

"Possibly."

"Because Harper was there?"

"Possibly."

"Because it happened to be nearby?"

"Possibly."

Jasper groaned.

Thorne almost smiled. "Mysteries are rarely polite enough to choose one theory because it is easier for us."

A wave broke far below.

Then the pressure touched Jasper's chest.

He stopped.

Thorne noticed immediately. "Now?"

Jasper nodded.

The feeling was faint and cool, more like being noticed from a distance than being called. He did not reach toward it.

"Anything else?" Thorne asked.

"No."

"Then you are allowed to leave it there."

Jasper tried.

For almost a minute, he managed.

The pressure shifted.

An image touched him.

Harper falling.

Not her face. Not even the whole ledge. Motion, stone, open air, then water rising fast enough to erase the gap beneath her.

Jasper's breath caught.

The image changed.

He saw a small figure standing beside the broken edge.

Himself.

The angle was wrong.

Jasper was looking up at himself from below.

The impression vanished.

He stumbled backward and Thorne caught his arm.

"What happened?"

Jasper told him. The words came too quickly. Harper falling. Water rising. Jasper standing above. The strange viewpoint.

When he finished, Thorne had gone very still.

"It saw me," Jasper whispered.

"That is one explanation."

"It remembered."

"That is another."

"But you think it did."

Thorne did not answer immediately. "I think what you experienced is stronger evidence than anything you had yesterday."

Jasper looked through the window again.

The sea appeared perfectly ordinary.

No voice named itself. No creature broke the surface. Nothing announced that Jasper had solved the mystery.

He had not.

But one possibility had become harder to ignore.

Whatever existed in the water might not simply react.

It might have a point of view.

CHAPTER THIRTY
HARPER GOES BACK
