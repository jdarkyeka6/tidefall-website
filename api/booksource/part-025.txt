 meant for Jasper. That mattered. Thorne told him about it because the writer had given permission for one part to be shared. Jasper sat in the Headmaster's study after lunch. No Harper. No Ava. Not because they were excluded from a danger. Because this conversation belonged partly to another person's privacy. Jasper had learned the difference. Thorne held a folded sheet.

Jasper stared at him. That was not the sentence he expected. The first had been clear. If it answers you, do not mistake being heard for being obeyed. This one felt less like a rule. More like something learned late.

Annoying. Probably correct. Jasper looked toward the window. The Sea glittered beyond it. For months he had thought about Deepwater as warning. A child who heard something ancient. Tested it. Mistook response for control. Someone got hurt. Rules changed. That story had become the shadow behind every choice Jasper made. Don't test. Don't reach alone. Don't assume. Don't control. Useful lessons. But a life made entirely of avoiding someone else's mistake would still be shaped by that mistake.

That might have been the most useful Deepwater fact Jasper ever learned. The story had always frozen its communicator at the worst moment. A mistake near a dock. An injury. Shame. But the child had grown up. Lived decades afterward. Water had not consumed their entire identity. Neither had the mistake.

Ava sighed. The sentence followed Jasper into the final week. Not a warning. Permission. He could be careful without becoming afraid of himself. He could listen without making listening the only thing he was. He could enter Choosing Tide without treating every movement of water as a test he might fail. Deepwater belonged in the school's memory. It did not need to own his future. Deepwater belonged in Tidefall's memory. It did not need to own Jasper's future.

CHAPTER EIGHTY-TWO
ORDER CLOTH

The academy gave first-years grey scarves before Choosing Tide. This confused Harper.

"Why give us a scarf in summer?"

The seamstress looked over her glasses.

"It is ceremonial cloth."

"Still hot."

"You are not required to wrap it around your neck."

"Then why is it a scarf?"

Ava pulled Harper away before the conversation became philosophical. The grey cloth had one purpose. After Choosing, it would be edged in the colour of the student's Order and become part of their Year Two uniform. Before Choosing, every scarf was plain. Same wool. Same length. Same small Tidefall crest stitched near one corner. Jasper held his up. It felt heavier than a strip of cloth should. Not magically. Meaning did that sometimes. The first-years collected them in the old sewing room, where shelves held boxes labelled by year and Order. Brannor trim. Velmor. Calyx. Nocten. The colors were not the colors of the students themselves, despite half the class pretending they were fortune signs.

Theo held his grey scarf against each box in turn.

"I look best in Velmor."

Mia said, "You look like a person holding grey fabric."

"Cruel."

Students also chose whether they wanted trousers, shorts, skirts or combinations for the next year's uniform set. Tidefall cared more about practical movement and weather than making every student look identical. Harper chose shorts immediately. Ava chose two skirts and trousers. Jasper chose trousers and one pair of formal shorts for warmer days. The seamstress measured sleeves and hems while students fidgeted.

"Stand still."

Harper stood still for three seconds.

"That was excellent," Jasper said.

"Thank you."

The seamstress did not share his appreciation. Afterward they carried their grey scarves back to Room Fourteen. Ava folded hers. Jasper placed his in his trunk. Harper tied hers around the Bag Bed's bedpost like a flag.

"That's not what it's for," Ava said.

"Temporary purpose."

The room had almost been packed. Their trunks stood open. Shelves were half empty. The Bag Bed no longer held mountains of shared overflow. Only the history box, handball trophy, tiny lift, a few final letters and Harper's grey scarf remained.

"We need to decide what happens to the history box," Jasper said.

Harper stopped. The box had begun as a joke. A container for things too ridiculous or important to throw away. Inside: The first Chronicle draft. A broken practice feather. Ava's old sock label. The replacement screw from the failed safety hook. A copy of the storm shelter schedule. A smooth pebble from the supervised tide-pool trip. Harper's pink handball final ticket. Theo's FOOD MISTAKES card. A paper model of the Choosing Basin. Nothing valuable. Everything valuable.

"Take it with us," Harper said.

Ava looked at her.

"What if we're in different Orders?"

Silence. The question had returned. Harper picked at the scarf knot.

"Then... library?"

"We could rotate it."

"Every week?"

"Every term."

"That's worse."

"Safe storage?"

"Done."

Harper hovered.

"You aren't going to read it?"

"No."

Harper turned.

"What?"

Ava made a sound of pain. Outside, the evening stayed warm long after sunset. Jasper carried his own scarf over one shoulder. Plain grey. Unchosen. For a few more days, all of them were.

CHAPTER EIGHTY-THREE
THE KITCHENS

The final Monday of first year began with Harper burning porridge. This required explanation because Harper was not cooking porridge. Nobody was. The first-years had been invited into Tidefall's kitchens for a year-end breakfast lesson. The kitchen staff believed students should leave first year knowing at least three things: How to make toast without magic. How to make tea without boiling the pot dry. How to prepare one simple breakfast if the dining hall was ever unavailable. Harper had been assigned fruit. Somehow, a nearby porridge pot still burned. Chef Marlow looked at the blackened base. Then at Harper.

"Explain."

Harper held a banana.

"I cannot."

Ava stood beside her with a knife and cutting board.

"She attempted Emberwarm on a spoon because it was wet."

"It was cold."

"Spoons are allowed to be cold."

"Not that one."

The heat charm had bounced off the metal and warmed the pot from below. Chef Marlow closed his eyes.

"First-years."

Jasper was making toast. He had made toast at home. This gave him an unreasonable sense of superiority. Soren burned his first piece. Jasper turned the rack at the correct moment. Perfect.

"Expert," Harper said.

"Thank you."

"A true wizard."

"Why?"

The student looked ashamed.

"Magic."

"Correct. That is what you used. I asked why."

Jasper tried not to laugh. They carried breakfast into a smaller side hall normally used by staff. Twenty-seven first-years squeezed around tables. The food was uneven. Some eggs were watery. "First spell."

"The storm."

"Handball."

"Deepwater."

The room quieted. Harper looked at the student who said it. He looked embarrassed.

Harper considered.

"It's fine. It happened."

She ate a strawberry.

"But I remember winning handball more."

Mia threw a napkin at her. The room laughed again. Jasper understood what she had done. Not denied the fall. Not let it become the first sentence of her year. When his turn came, he said, "The first Sunday letters." Everyone looked surprised. Harper said, "Really?"

She would say it counted. The last school week was full of tiny endings like that. Not final enough to hurt. Just enough to notice.

CHAPTER EIGHTY-FOUR
THE WEST STAIR RACE

Harper had wanted to race the West Stair since the first week of school. The rule was simple. Students were allowed to time themselves climbing from the lower residence arch to the upper courtyard only during the supervised year-end stair race. No running on ordinary days. No jumping railings. No magic. No skipping steps. The event existed, according to staff, because students were going to race anyway and Tidefall preferred ambulances not be part of the tradition. Harper considered this the wisest decision the academy had ever made. Ava did not enter.

"Why not?"

"I do not enjoy stairs competitively."

"Weak."

"Alive."

Jasper entered because Harper wrote his name on the list before asking.

"Consent," he said.

She froze. Then immediately crossed his name out.

"Sorry."

Jasper looked at the sheet. He had been teasing. But she had actually stopped. That mattered.

"I do want to race."

"Then put yourself back."

He did. Another small change. The race ran in heats of four. Students started at the lower arch. Two prefects stood halfway. Ward waited at the top with a timing slate and medical kit.

"This says a lot about the event," Ava observed.

Harper bounced on her toes.

"It says they respect sport."

"It says they expect sprains."

Jasper's heat came first. He was not fast. He knew this. Still, the moment Ward called start, something competitive entered his body that had apparently been hiding all year. He ran. The stair curved. Stone steps hammered under shoes. Theo went ahead immediately. Jasper stayed behind Mia. At the halfway turn, Soren tried to take two steps at once and nearly lost balance. Jasper caught his arm. Both slowed.

"Go!" Soren said.

Jasper released him once he was steady. Theo finished first. Mia second. Jasper third. Soren fourth. Jasper bent over breathing hard. Harper met him at the top.

"You stopped."

"He nearly fell."

"I saw."

Jasper waited for her to say he should have kept running. She punched his shoulder lightly.

"Good."

Then it was Harper's turn. The whole first-year cohort knew who would probably win. Harper knew too. That made Jasper nervous for her. Confidence could become weight. She lined up beside Benji, Leila and another girl called Freya. Ava stood beside Jasper with the timing sheet.

"Her best practice stair time is forty-nine seconds."

"Why do you know that?"

"She told me. Repeatedly."

At the second landing Freya was almost beside her. Harper looked back. Ava shouted, "FORWARD." Harper snapped her head around. Jasper laughed. Final stair. Harper reached the courtyard first. Ward tapped the slate.

"I have four years."

Ava said, "That is not an invitation to train on unsupervised stairs."

"I know."

"Say it like you mean it."

"I know."

"Thanks."

"For what?"

"Stair."

"You were fine."

"Would've been less fine if my face met step thirty-one."

Jasper smiled.

"Any time."

Soren looked at him.

"Hopefully not any time."

"Good point."

That evening Harper put her race ribbon in the history box temporarily returned for the final week. The box was becoming full.

"We need a bigger box next year," she said.

Ava stopped folding clothes. Next year. Not if we're together. Not if we end up somewhere convenient. Just next year. Harper seemed to realize after saying it. She looked at both of them.

"We'll still have a box."

Jasper nodded.

"Yeah."

Ava said, "Obviously." Nobody discussed where it would live. For once they let the future wait.
