CHAPTER SEVENTY-FIVE
THE LAST MAGIC SHOWCASE

The first practical spell Jasper had ever cast at Tidefall had made a feather wobble. He had been proud of it. Harper's feather had shot off the desk. Ava's had risen exactly four centimetres. Months later, their final Year One magic showcase asked them to do something much harder. Not powerful. Controlled. Professor Ward explained the exercise in the practice hall.

"Each of you will complete three tasks. Movement. Shielding. Adaptation."

Harper raised a hand.

"Is there a score?"

"Yes."

"Excellent."

"Against your own earlier assessments."

Harper lowered her hand.

"Less excellent."

The movement task involved carrying five small wooden discs through a course without touching them. Not all at once. Students could choose how many to lift. The temptation was obvious. Harper tried three. One flew sideways. She caught it with her hand before it hit Theo. Ward raised an eyebrow.

"Hands are not disqualifying in life. They are disqualifying in this exercise."

"Worth it."

Her second attempt used two discs. Slower. Clean. Jasper chose one at a time. He could probably manage two. He decided not to prove it. The course included a narrow gate and moving bar. His disc drifted too high. Jasper corrected gently. Passed. Ava attempted two and made it through until the last turn, where one disc clipped a marker. She froze. Old Ava would have restarted. Instead she lowered the damaged disc, continued with the other and completed the course. Ward wrote something. Ava looked suspicious.

"What?"

"Adaptation."

"This is movement."

"Everything is adaptation if you wait long enough."

"That is not useful marking."

Ward smiled. Shielding came next. Pairs stood behind low marked lines while soft practice pulses struck from different directions. Jasper had become good at shields. Not huge ones. Not dramatic walls. His strength was placement. A small shield exactly where needed. He paired with Mia. First pulse left. Jasper caught it. Second high. Mia caught it. Third came low and split in two. Jasper started to cover both. Then remembered Harper's lesson. Protection was not doing everyone else's job for them. He took his side. Mia took hers. Both held.

"Good," Ward said.

Jasper knew she meant more than the spell. Harper's shield looked like Harper. Strong. Fast. Too large. Her first pulse hit and sprayed sparks across half the room because she had overpowered the response. She frowned. Second pulse. Smaller shield. Third. Smaller again. By the last, her barrier appeared only where the pulse struck. Harper stared at it.

"That's boring."

Ava said, "It worked."

"Exactly. Suspicious."

The adaptation task was the hardest. Every student received a problem with missing information. Jasper's involved transporting a cup of water across a table while one spell category was unavailable. He read the card twice. MOVEMENT SPELLS DISABLED. Harper leaned over from her station.

"Drink it and walk."

Ward pointed at her own task.

"Miss Vale."

Harper returned to it. Jasper looked at the cup. The obvious magical answer was shielding. Create a sliding barrier. Too complicated. He could simply pick up the cup. The instructions did not say magic was required. He carried it to the other end. Ward stared. Jasper waited.

"Correct."

Harper yelled from across the room.

"I SAID THAT."

Her own card required lighting a lamp without Emberwarm. She had spent three minutes trying increasingly complicated light charms. Ava walked past and struck an ordinary match from the supply tray. Harper stared.

"This whole assessment is anti-magic propaganda."

"It is anti-unnecessary-complexity propaganda," Ward said.

Ava's problem removed written notes during a route-planning task. At first she looked personally betrayed. Then she asked another student to remember one half while she remembered the other. That was her adaptation. Not perfect memory. Shared memory. At the end, Ward returned their first assessment cards from the beginning of the year. Jasper read his. MOVEMENT: hesitant. SHIELD: late response. ADAPTATION: asks for confirmation before continuing. Now: MOVEMENT: controlled. SHIELD: precise. ADAPTATION: recognizes when non-magical solution is sufficient. He liked that last one best. Harper's new card said: POWER IS NOT THE SAME AS EFFECTIVENESS. She accused Ward of writing a personal attack. Ava's said:

UNCERTAINTY NO LONGER STOPS ACTION. No ranking. Some were pleased. Some disappointed. Tidefall was not perfect. But it was trying. That mattered too.

Ava read it silently for a while. Then folded the card carefully and put it in her pocket. After the showcase, students gathered in the courtyard with juice and icy poles. No awards for strongest spell. Every student received one comment about growth and one skill to take into Year Two. Jasper watched first-years compare cards. One boy had clearly expected a higher result and was trying not to cry. Ashcroft sat beside him on a low wall and talked quietly. It had old secrets and bad records and walls built over things people no longer understood. Harper came beside Jasper with a blue icy pole. Her tongue was already blue.

"What'd you get?"

He showed her. She read the last line.

"Non-magical solution."

"Yep."

"You carried a cup."

"Expertly."

"Heroic."

Ava joined them.

"He did not spill it."

"Thank you, Ava."

"That was not praise."

The Sea was visible beyond the courtyard arches. Jasper did not look for it. Today the magic that mattered fit inside a practice hall. That felt good.

CHAPTER SEVENTY-SIX
THE MYTHS PEOPLE MAKE

Three days after Open Week, every first-year suddenly knew a story about Choosing Tide. None of the stories agreed. According to Theo, refusing to move caused the basin to dump you into Nocten automatically. According to Mia, her cousin's friend's older sister had been held in the middle for eleven minutes while all four Orders "argued." According to Harper, the Tide once launched a boy directly over a platform because he insulted Brannor. Ava rejected all three.

"Source."

Harper pointed vaguely upward.

"Culture."

"That is not a source."

"It is if enough people repeat it."

"That is how misinformation works."

Jasper listened to the argument from his bed. The closer the ceremony came, the stranger the rumours became. Some students claimed the water read your thoughts. Others said the Tide chose based on the first spell you ever cast.

One first-year insisted it was secretly random and the academy only pretended otherwise. A Nocten third-year told everyone the basin could smell fear. Then admitted he had made that up. Thorne finally called a first-year meeting. He stood at the front of the hall beside a model of the basin.

"We appear to have reached the annual stage at which imagination overtakes plumbing."

Harper whispered, "Great opening." Thorne continued.

"Choosing Tide is old. It is magical. Its precise original design is not fully documented. Those facts do not give you permission to invent sharks."

Theo looked disappointed.

"No student has been eaten. No student has been thrown over an Order platform. No Order can reject a student. No student can be left without an Order."

A hand went up.

"Can the Tide change its mind?"

Thorne paused.

"The current may hesitate or redirect. The final platform is the placement."

Another hand.

"Can you fight it?"

A teacher at the side of the room closed her eyes. Thorne said, "You should not." Harper whispered, "Not cannot." Ava pinched her arm.

"Ow."

Thorne's eyes flicked toward them.

"The basin is shallow. Staff are present. If a child becomes frightened or distressed, the ceremony stops. Safety is more important than tradition."

Jasper liked that sentence. He wondered whether it had always been true. Probably not. Tidefall kept traditions, but the year had taught him traditions could improve. Thorne gestured to the model.

"The academy's working moves the water through four channels. It reacts to a combination of magical signature, sustained choices, patterns of learning and factors we do not fully understand."

Ava raised her hand.

"So it is not personality detection."

"Correct."

"Not elemental affinity."

"Correct."

"Not strongest subject."

"Correct."

"Not family inheritance."

"Correct. Relatives frequently enter different Orders."

Ava looked satisfied. Harper raised her hand.

"Does the actual Sea choose?"

The room changed. Jasper felt several students look at him. Thorne did not.

"The basin uses seawater," he said. "The ritual predates much of our current understanding. We have no evidence that an independent presence in the Sea personally assigns every student."

Precise. Not denial. Not claiming more than they knew. Harper asked, "Could it interfere?" This time Thorne looked at Jasper. Only briefly.

"We do not know."

Whispers spread. Jasper hated them instantly. After the meeting, two students approached him before he reached the door.

"Can you ask it where we're going?"

"No."

"But can you feel what it'll choose?"

"No."

"Could you try?"

"No."

Harper appeared at his side.

"He said no."

The students left. Jasper was grateful. Then annoyed that he was grateful. He looked at Harper.

"I could answer."

"You did."

"You jumped in."

"They kept asking."

Ava joined them.

"Both things can be true."

Harper groaned.

"Is this the protection conversation again?"

"Yes."

Jasper exhaled.

"Thanks. But let me handle the first two nos."

Harper considered.

"Third no I bite them?"

"No biting."

"Unreasonable."

That afternoon the rumour changed. Now Jasper supposedly knew everyone's Order and had been forbidden to say. He discovered this when Theo asked him whether he was "under oath."

"No."

Theo nodded knowingly.

"Exactly what someone under oath would say."

Jasper threw an eraser at him. By dinner, he had become tired enough to consider hiding in the library. Thorne found him first. Not deliberately, Jasper thought. The Headmaster was crossing the east cloister carrying a stack of folders. He stopped.

"You look hunted."

"Everyone thinks I can predict Orders."

"Can you?"

Jasper stared. Thorne's mouth moved slightly. A joke. Terrible timing.

"No."

"Good. That would create paperwork."

Jasper leaned against a pillar.

"What if Water does something during mine?"

Thorne's expression lost the joke.

"Then we observe."

"What if everyone thinks I caused it?"

"Then we correct them."

"What if I think I caused it?"

Thorne shifted the folders to one arm.

"Then remember the year you have just spent learning the difference between invitation, communication and control."

Jasper looked toward the sea.

"I won't ask it."

"Good."

"Even if it shows up."

"Good."

"What if it chooses something I don't want?"

Thorne took longer to answer.

"That question applies to the academy ritual whether Water appears or not."

Jasper nodded.

"You're allowed to dislike an outcome," Thorne said. "Belonging is not obedience. Orders are meant to give you a community, not erase your judgment."

Jasper thought of Brannor's buckets. Velmor's broken map. Calyx's soil. Nocten's witness statements. All four had room for him. Maybe that was the point. The ceremony would choose a direction. It would not write the rest of his life. That evening, Room Fourteen made a new rule. No more Order predictions after lights-out. Harper broke it in six minutes.

"Ava is absolutely Nocten."

A pillow hit her in the face. The rule improved immediately.

CHAPTER SEVENTY-SEVEN
THE NIGHT MARKET

Tidefall held the Night Market on the second-last Friday of every school year. Nobody had mentioned this to the first-years until two days before it happened. Harper considered that criminal.

"You kept a market secret?"

Professor Elowen looked up from attendance.

"It was on the calendar."

"Calendars are where adults hide things."

Ava said, "I read the calendar."

"Exactly."

The Night Market transformed Founders' Courtyard after sunset. Strings of floating lanterns hung between arches. Tables appeared under striped awnings. Older students sold things they had made during the year. Enchanted bookmarks that remembered your page. Tiny ceramic pots warmed by gentle heat charms. Braided bracelets in Order colours. Wooden puzzles. Caramel slices from the kitchens. Second-hand books. Postcards painted with the academy cliffs. No real money changed hands inside the student market. Students used small stamped tokens issued for the evening, enough that nobody's family income decided whether they could take part. Ava approved of this system immediately. Harper approved of the food. Jasper liked the noise. It was different from the dining hall. Less organized.

Music came from three older students under the west arch. Someone had enchanted paper fish to swim through the air above a craft stall. First-years wandered in groups, pretending they were independent while staff stood at every exit. Room Fourteen received twelve tokens each. Harper spent three within four minutes.

"You have no restraint," Ava said.

Harper held up a bag of cinnamon popcorn.

"Counterpoint."

They visited Soren's table first. He and two other students had restored pieces of old Tidefall machinery into desk toys. A tiny counterweight lift moved a wooden platform six centimetres when you turned a brass wheel. Jasper stared.

"You made that?"

"Mostly. Ashcroft stopped me using an original nineteenth-century gear because apparently 'historical preservation' matters."

"It does."

"Traitor."

Jasper bought one. It cost four tokens. Worth it. Mia's table sold painted stones from the permitted inland collection area. Not magical. Just good. Harper bought a pink one with a tiny white wave painted across it. Mia looked at her.

"You know that's a wave."

"Yes."

"You spent half the year pretending the Sea thing was coincidence."

"Character development."

"Fair."

Ava spent two tokens on a set of green page markers and then fifteen minutes deciding whether the purchase had been frivolous. Harper bought her another set purely to end the crisis.

"You cannot solve decision discomfort by buying both options," Ava said.

"Watch me."

At the northern end of the market, the Orders ran community stalls. Not recruitment. That rule was written on a sign large enough that Harper read it aloud sarcastically. Brannor repaired broken bag straps and sharpened kitchen knives for staff. Velmor offered weather postcards showing tomorrow's likely cloud patterns. Calyx gave out herb cuttings and taught students how to keep them alive over summer. Nocten ran a lost-property identification game that was probably an excuse to return three months of abandoned belongings. Jasper found one of his own socks there. He did not ask how.

"Buy it back," Harper said.

"It is mine."

"Then steal it."

A Nocten prefect looked up. Jasper took the sock and left. The market should have been entirely ordinary. For most of the evening, it was. Then Jasper saw the shell. It sat on a second-hand trinket table run by seventh-years. Small. White. Spiral-shaped. Nothing magical about it from a distance. Jasper stopped. A faint cool pressure touched his ribs. Not strong. Water. Harper noticed first.

"What?"

Jasper looked at the shell. The Sea's attention did not feel alarmed. More like recognition. He approached. The older student behind the table smiled.

"Two tokens. Came out of a storage box from the old harbour rooms."

Ava immediately said, "Was it cleared for sale?"

"Yes. Crowe checked everything."

Jasper did not touch it. The shell was ordinary except for one thin blue line painted inside the spiral. A harbour marker. Old. He had seen the same colour in archive photographs of the lower docks before Deepwater. Ava recognized it too.

"That's an old safety mark."

The seventh-year shrugged.

"Probably. The shell itself isn't enchanted."

Jasper felt Water again. A tiny impression. Stone steps wet with rain. A hand holding the shell. Then nothing. A memory? Not his. Maybe not Water's either. He stepped back.

"I don't want it."

Harper looked surprised. Months ago Jasper would have bought it. Taken it apart. Asked questions. Tried to find the person in the impression. Now he knew better than to turn every piece of attention into a demand for investigation. Ava asked, "Should we tell Crowe?" Jasper thought.

"Only what happened. Not that something's wrong."

They found Crowe near the staff tea table. Jasper described the impression. Crowe listened.

"Any sense of danger?"

"No."

"Any instruction?"

"No."

"Then thank you for reporting it. I'll recheck the item before it leaves school property."
