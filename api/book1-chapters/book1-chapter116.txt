The history box sat on Jasper's bedroom floor for two days before he opened it. His mother respected this. His father nearly did.

"What's in it?"

"Stuff."

"Dangerous stuff?"

"A broken feather."

"Could be dangerous."

"Dad."

"Leaving."

He did. Home felt smaller than Jasper remembered, but not in a bad way. The room had not changed. Jasper had simply acquired another scale for comparison. On the third evening, he opened the box. The contents were ridiculous when spread across the carpet.

A broken feather from Foundations. The west-stair pebble. A yellow bookmark. A folded emergency card. The first safety hook prototype. Harper's race ribbon. Ava's old measuring sheet. Three Brannor stones. The First Saturday card. A copy of the Chronicle page titled THINGS YOU ARE ALLOWED NOT TO KNOW YET. His mum appeared at the doorway and sat beside him.

"That all from one year?"

"Most of it."

She picked up the faded room rules. "You kept rules?"

"Ava."

"Of course."

Jasper put the objects back slowly. He did not want to display everything on shelves. That felt too much like trying to keep the year permanently open. The history box slid under his bed. Not hidden. Stored.

He did. Home felt smaller than Jasper remembered. Not worse. Closer. His bed did not creak. The bathroom was three steps away instead of down a corridor. Breakfast did not involve two hundred children. Nobody rang a bell before lessons because there were no lessons. On the first morning Jasper woke at six forty and got dressed before remembering summer existed. His mother found him in the kitchen wearing proper clothes.

She laughed. By day three he remembered how home worked. Shoes left near the door. Radio on during breakfast. His father losing keys. His mother asking whether he wanted to come to the shops. No schedule board. No Order duties. No Sea pressure. That last one remained strange. Their town was coastal. He could see ocean from a nearby rise. He went there with his parents on Saturday. Ordinary waves. Ordinary salt. Jasper waited without intending to wait. Nothing. He did not test. He did not think move. He simply noticed the absence. His mother watched him.

She nodded. They bought fish and chips afterward and ate near the beach. A gull stole one chip from his father. Jasper laughed so hard he forgot about Water. At home that evening, he opened the history box. One piece at a time. Broken practice feather. First Chronicle draft. Harper's race ribbon. Ava's original sock label. Storm schedule copy. Brannor stones. Anonymous thank-you cards. Tiny paper rules from first week. His mother sat on the floor outside the box's reach.

His mother laughed. He did not show the anonymous cards. Did not show Ava's sock label because it was funnier as private Room Fourteen history. Did not show every storm note. He did show the Chronicle. His mother read THINGS YOU ARE ALLOWED NOT TO KNOW YET. She got to the line about problems you cannot solve alone.

No planning. No panic. He would. That night he put the box under his desk. Not displayed like a trophy. Not hidden. Stored. First year was not something he needed to stare at every morning. It was something he carried forward when useful. First year was not something he needed to stare at every morning. It was something he could carry forward when useful.
