The lower harbour reopened twelve days after the storm. Year One visited under supervision as part of the investigation review. Professor Crowe believed students who had sheltered through the emergency should see what had actually happened instead of building it from rumours. Harper approved. Ava called this good risk communication. Jasper called it a field trip. All three were right. The repaired lower door opened normally. Orange life jackets returned. Harper complained less this time. The harbour looked smaller in daylight than it had inside Jasper's storm impressions. That was the first surprise. The gate was large, but not enormous. The breakwater was thick, but not invincible. The inner platform had two new rail sections where the surge tore old ones away.

Scrape marks crossed one stone wall. Several mooring posts had been replaced. The work barge was absent, still being repaired south of Tidefall. Crowe gathered the class behind the yellow line.

"No part of this harbour survived because one person saved it," she said.

Harper whispered, "She hates hero stories." Ava said, "Correctly." Forecast change. Storm preparation. Gate obstruction. Harbour evacuation. Mooring failure. Ava's timing estimate. Jasper's report. Final clearance. Structural response. Crowe walked them through the sequence.

"Why tell us all this?" Theo asked.

"Because emergency systems only work if people understand them. Also because some of you have invented a story involving Jasper standing on the roof commanding waves."

Several students looked at the floor. Harper snorted. Crowe pointed to the gate.

"The gate jammed because broken timber lodged against the north hinge. The surge cleared it. Jasper reported an unusual sensation before the surge. That is the extent of what most of you need to know."

Jasper appreciated that too. Private, not secret. Different. The group moved to the upper observation platform. Crowe showed the damaged guide ring from Jasper's earlier dream. The hairline crack had widened during load.

"Why wasn't it found?" Ava asked.

"Because inspections are not perfect. We are changing the test method."

Again, academy responsibility rather than pretending systems never failed. Jasper looked through the open gate. The Titan-presence was faint. Nothing like the storm. He felt the shape of water passing through the harbour. Open. No constriction. The word was his, not the Sea's. He was learning to label carefully. Harper stood beside him.

"Does it feel happy?"

"I don't know if happy is right."

"Fine?"

"Open."

"That's not a feeling."

"Exactly."

Harper rolled her eyes. Ava came over.

"Can you feel the gate?"

"Kind of. Through water pressure."

"Could you map it?"

Jasper looked at her.

"Maybe."

Ava reached for the notebook. Then stopped.

"Only if you want."

Jasper smiled.

"Later."

The class practised rescue lines again. This time Jasper hit the target on his second throw. Harper hit it first but almost stepped over the yellow line while celebrating. Ava grabbed her jacket. Harper froze. Then laughed.

"Reflex."

"Good reflex."

At the end of the lesson, Crowe allowed the trio to stand with her near the inner control panel. The repaired gate lever was locked inside a protective box.

"During the storm," Crowe said, "we had two bad options. Leave the gate jammed and let pressure increase, or attempt manual clearance in conditions no person should enter. We chose not to risk people."

Harper nodded.

"Good."

"That meant accepting possible damage."

Ava looked at the new rails.

"Property over people."

"People over property," Crowe corrected.

"That's what I meant."

Crowe smiled. Jasper looked at the gate.

"Did you wait because I said the surge was coming?"

"We evacuated faster because of your report and Ava's intervals. We would have evacuated anyway once line three failed."

Jasper nodded.

"Good."

Crowe looked at him.

"You wanted to know whether we depended on you."

"Yeah."

"We used information you had. We did not hand you responsibility for the harbour."

Exactly what Thorne promised. The class climbed back to Tidefall before lunch. Harper complained about stairs again. Some things survived every crisis.
