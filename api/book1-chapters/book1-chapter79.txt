Ava found her own sentence in the new safety handbook. Not exactly. The wording had changed. But she knew where it came from.

IF AN EVENT CANNOT YET BE EXPLAINED, RECORD OBSERVATIONS SEPARATELY FROM INTERPRETATION.

Ava read it three times. Then took the handbook to Crowe. Crowe was in the archive office surrounded by boxes. The room smelled like paper, dust and whatever tea she forgot to drink. Ava placed the handbook on her desk.

"This is mine."

Crowe read the line.

"The concept is not exclusively yours."

"I wrote nearly that exact sentence in my Deepwater notes."

Crowe looked up.

"Would you like attribution in the safety manual?"

Ava considered.

"No."

"Then what are we discussing?"

Ava sat.

"I don't know."

That was rare enough that Crowe put down her pen. Ava looked at the boxes.

"Did the school really forget Deepwater?"

"No."

"Then why were the records so bad?"

Crowe leaned back.

"Because forgetting is not the only way institutions lose knowledge."

Ava waited.

"Sometimes a person knows and retires. Sometimes a record is sealed for a good reason and nobody writes the safe version that could remain open. Sometimes people are embarrassed. Sometimes they believe a dangerous event is unique and not worth teaching broadly. Sometimes everyone assumes someone else remembers the important part."

Ava looked at the handbook.

"So the information exists but stops moving."

"Exactly."

"Like a blocked tide gate."

Crowe smiled slightly.

"Jasper has infected your metaphors."

"Unfortunately."

Crowe opened one of the boxes. Inside were copies of first-year incident reports from decades of school history. Minor burns. Falls. Broken equipment. Spell mistakes. Most boring. Boring was good.

"We are changing the archive system," Crowe said. "Privacy layers. Safety summaries. Clear retention rules. Events can remain confidential without becoming unusable."

Ava looked at her.

"Because of Deepwater."

"Because of Deepwater, your questions, the storm and several other cases you will never hear about because they are not your business."

Ava appreciated that.

"Can I help?"

Crowe raised an eyebrow.

"You are seven."

"I can alphabetize."

"Dangerously well."

Crowe gave her one safe task. Checking the new student-facing safety summaries for confusing language. Nothing private. Nothing sealed. Just sentences meant for children. Ava spent two hours finding problems.

"'Do not access restricted areas' is useless if the document doesn't define restricted."

Crowe changed it.

"'Seek staff assistance as appropriate' is vague."

Changed.

"This says 'avoid excessive magical exertion.' How much is excessive?"

Crowe sighed.

"That one requires medical guidance. Mark it."

Ava did. By dinner she had covered five pages in green annotations. Crowe looked exhausted.

"You asked."

"I did."

"Regret?"

"Some."

Ava smiled. That evening she told Harper and Jasper about the handbook. Harper was lying upside down on her bed.

"You edited school rules for fun."

"Not rules. Safety guidance."

"Worse."

Jasper picked up the draft. One new paragraph concerned unusual magical sensitivities. STUDENTS ARE NOT REQUIRED TO DEMONSTRATE, REPRODUCE OR USE UNUSUAL SENSORY OR MAGICAL ABILITIES FOR STAFF CURIOSITY, LESSONS OR RESEARCH. SAFETY-CRITICAL INFORMATION SHOULD BE REPORTED, BUT PARTICIPATION IN FURTHER TESTING REQUIRES CONSENT AND GUARDIAN INVOLVEMENT. His mother's fingerprints were all over the idea, even if not the wording. He read it twice.

"This is good."

Ava nodded.

"Crowe said it came from the post-storm review."

Harper flipped upright.

"So if some teacher says 'Jasper, make the ocean do the thing,' he can tell them to get stuffed."

Ava looked pained.

"Not the exact language."

"Spiritually."

Jasper smiled.

"I already could."

"Now there's paperwork supporting you."

Ava said this with deep satisfaction. Jasper laughed. The storm would not. Harper's fall would not. Benji's fall would not. Hooks. Consent rules. Clear histories. Updated harbour procedures. Maybe schools were too.

The safety handbook was not dramatic. Nobody would write legends about it. That was why it mattered. Deepwater had become a story because people failed to turn lessons into systems. The school remembered by changing what it did next. Jasper thought of the Chronicle line. You are allowed not to know yet. But once they learned something, they had to decide whether to carry it forward.
