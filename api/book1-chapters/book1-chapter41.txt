Professor Ward took the first-years inland. Not far. Only beyond the final academy ridge where the ocean disappeared from view. Jasper had not realised how strange that would feel. The excursion began after breakfast with twenty-three children, four staff members and enough water bottles to survive a desert crossing. Harper complained about the hill. Ava reminded her she had run the Brannor obstacle course twice voluntarily.

"That had obstacles."

"This has hill."

"Exactly. Boring obstacle."

The path climbed through coastal scrub, then dipped into a broad valley of eucalypts and pale grass. Birds replaced gulls. The air smelled dry instead of salty. No sea anywhere. The lesson was environmental magic away from the coast. Professor Ward wanted them to understand that Tidefall's location could distort what felt normal.

"You have spent most of your magical education surrounded by stone wards and marine background magic," she said. "Today you notice what changes when those disappear."

Students spread out in groups. They tested simple sensing exercises. Not sensing magical creatures. Background pressure. Where old tree roots held faint traces of growth enchantment from decades of student visits. Where a creek carried almost no magical charge at all. Where a weathered marker stone still held a weak orientation spell. Jasper expected to feel the Titan-presence faintly to the west. He did not. Nothing. The absence was complete. He sat under a tree with Ava while Harper attempted to convince Mia that climbing one particular low branch was educational.

"Gone?" Ava asked.

"Yeah."

"At what point?"

"Somewhere over the ridge."

"Distance or line of sight?"

"No idea."

Ava nodded. She did not reach for the notebook. Jasper noticed.

"You can write it."

"I know."

"Why aren't you?"

"Because we're eating."

Progress again. Lunch was sandwiches, apples and one squashed packet of biscuits Harper had packed badly. Soren read against a tree. Theo tried to make a grass blade stand upright using magic and accidentally lifted a clump of dirt. Professor Ward made him put it back. The afternoon exercise paired students with unfamiliar classmates. Jasper worked with Theo. Harper with Soren. Ava with Mia. They had to guide one another through a short marked path while one person wore a cloth over their eyes. No magic. Communication only. Jasper hated being blindfolded.

"Two steps forward," Theo said.

"Big steps?"

"Normal."

"Your normal or mine?"

"You're shorter."

"We're the same height."

"Really?"

Jasper stopped.

"This is not helping."

Theo laughed. They finished without walking into a tree. Harper's turn with Soren was worse.

"Left," Soren said.

Harper went left fast.

"Slow left."

"You didn't say slow."

"I thought slow was obvious when blindfolded."

"Nothing is obvious."

Ava and Mia finished first. Harper accused Ava of being a robot. Ava took it as a compliment. The walk back began near sunset. As they climbed the ridge, Jasper felt the Titan-presence return before the water became visible. Soft. Familiar. He stopped on the path. Professor Ward, walking behind, noticed.

"Sea?"

"Yeah."

"Strong?"

"No."

Ava looked toward the top of the ridge.

"We can't see it yet."

Jasper nodded. Ward looked thoughtful.

"Useful."

Harper said, "Ava's spreading." Ward smiled. They reached the ridge. Not greeting. Not calling. There. Nothing. Toward Tidefall. Presence. Not a wall. Range. The whole coast opened below them. Tidefall's towers shone pale in the late sun. The sea stretched beyond, blue and endless. Jasper felt the presence more clearly. He looked inland behind him. A boundary of some kind.

"Do you think it knows when I leave?" Jasper asked.

Ward considered.

"Do you know when an ant leaves your garden?"

Harper laughed. Jasper stared.

"Thanks."

Ward smiled.

"Scale matters. We do not know how much attention you occupy. Do not assume its awareness works like ours."

Jasper looked at the sea again. That was humbling. He had spent months thinking about whether the presence watched him. Maybe Jasper was one tiny sensation in something that held an entire coastline at once. Maybe it noticed him sometimes the way he noticed one bird call among hundreds. The thought made the mystery feel larger and less personal. He liked that. When they returned to Tidefall, the first thing Harper did was complain about the stairs. The second was raid Bag Bed for biscuits. The third was fall asleep before dinner study ended. A normal end to a day without the sea.
