Their first supervised trip beyond the main academy walls happened near the end of first term. Every first-year signed three safety forms. Every parent had already signed another. Harper said the amount of paperwork suggested they were going somewhere dangerous. Ava said the paperwork existed specifically to reduce that. Harper remained disappointed. They were going to tide pools.

"I thought beyond the walls meant adventure."

Professor Elowen adjusted the strap of her field bag.

"A slippery rock can provide more adventure than most people need."

The group descended by a broad inland path rather than cliff stairs. Two staff members walked ahead, two behind. They reached a protected cove south of the academy where a shelf of dark rock spread out at low tide. The ocean was farther away than Jasper expected. Pools remained trapped in hollows across the stone, each one holding tiny worlds. Anemones. Small crabs. Snails. Silver fish that vanished under ledges when shadows crossed the water. The lesson was part Ocean Studies, part environmental magic. Professor Elowen explained that natural systems often held background magic differently from classrooms.

"You are not here to cast at anything," she said. "You are here to notice where your senses end and assumptions begin."

Ava looked delighted again. Harper had found a crab.

"Can I pick it up?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because it lives here."

"Fair."

Jasper knelt beside a clear pool with Ava. They were supposed to sketch the shape and note temperature, water level and visible life. Jasper dipped the tip of the thermometer. The pool surface trembled. No wind. He looked up. Ava noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing."

"You keep saying that before things."

Jasper touched two fingers lightly to the rock beside the pool. No pressure. No deep presence. The water trembled again. Then a tiny crab emerged from under the edge. Jasper exhaled.

"Crab."

Ava stared.

"You thought it was the sea thing."

"For a second."

"This is why controls matter."

"Don't make my fear into homework."

She smiled. Farther along, Harper slipped. Again. This time she fell directly onto her backside in two centimetres of water. The entire group heard the splash. Mia laughed first. Then everyone. Harper sat in the pool, soaked from the waist down, expression blank. Jasper waited for the sea. Nothing. No pressure. No response. Professor Elowen helped Harper stand.

"Injured?"

"Only spiritually."

"Good. Drying charm when we return."

Harper squelched for the rest of the lesson. Jasper kept thinking about the lack of response. If the presence cared about Harper slipping on the west stair, why not now? Maybe it had not cared then. Maybe Jasper had imagined the pressure. Maybe the presence was not watching specific people at all. He hated how many maybes could fit inside one afternoon. At low tide, the group approached the outer edge of the shelf. Still far from deep water. A rope boundary marked the safe line. Beyond it, waves rolled into a narrow channel. Professor Elowen pointed toward dark holes in the cliff base farther north.

"Sea caves. Some connect under the academy. You will not enter them. Even older students require specific supervision."

Harper raised her hand.

"Has anyone?"

"Entered? Yes."

"Got lost?"

"Yes."

"Died?"

Elowen paused.

"Not during my time here."

Harper nodded as if this were a useful statistic. Ava whispered, "Why are you like this?" Jasper stared toward the nearest sea cave. The pressure touched him. Sudden. Deep. His vision blurred. For one instant he was not looking at the cave from outside. He was inside water. Dark rock passed on either side. A current moved through a submerged tunnel. Then an impression of bright open sea. The vision snapped. Jasper stumbled. Ava caught his arm. Professor Elowen was beside him immediately.

"Jasper?"

"I'm okay."

"What happened?"

He told her. Not everything felt easy to say with twenty other children nearby. But he told the truth. Dark tunnel. Current. Open sea. Elowen's face tightened.

"Any pain?"

"No."

"Dizziness?"

"Only for a second."

She sent another teacher to continue the lesson while she walked Jasper, Harper and Ava back toward the inner pools. Harper said, "We can go with the class."

"You can. Jasper is staying with me."

Harper stayed too. Ava did not even ask. Elowen crouched beside Jasper.

"Did you reach for it?"

"No."

"Think about it deliberately?"

"I was looking at the cave."

"That is not the same thing."

Jasper breathed out.

"Am I doing something wrong?"

"No."

"Then why does it keep happening?"

Professor Elowen looked toward the distant waves.

"I don't know."

Adults saying I don't know had stopped feeling like failure. Sometimes it was the most useful answer they gave him.

"Thorne should know," she added. "I'll tell him."

"Okay."

They sat until Jasper felt completely normal. On the walk back Harper's wet shorts had finally dried enough that she stopped complaining. Ava asked, "Did the tunnel feel real?"

"Yeah."

"Memory?"

"Maybe."

"Direction?"

"I don't know."

Harper said, "What if it was showing you a way in?" Jasper's stomach tightened. Ava said, "Or a way out." They both looked at her.

"What?"

Ava adjusted her field bag.

"Same image. Different assumption."

Jasper thought about that all the way home. He had seen a tunnel and decided invitation. Ava had seen the same story and decided escape. Maybe neither was right. Maybe communication was less about receiving answers and more about surviving how badly people wanted them.
