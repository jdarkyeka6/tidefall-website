The first-years spent almost an entire year at Tidefall before the Academy took them into Lowtide Town. Harper considered this evidence that the adults had been hiding something excellent. Professor Elowen considered it evidence that seven-year-olds required several months of basic safety training before being taken anywhere interesting.

Lowtide lay several kilometres south of the Academy, although maps were not especially helpful once the road entered the concealed coastal zone. The town was still physically in Australia. The ocean beside it was the same ocean Jasper could see from ordinary beaches near Perth, and a determined person with the correct route could travel between Lowtide and the outside world without crossing into another realm. The strange part was the space in between.

Tidefall's old concealment magic worked in two layers. The first was perceptual: roads looked less inviting than they were, distant buildings seemed unimportant, and people without permission tended to remember perfectly reasonable reasons to turn around. The second was geographical magic so old that even teachers disagreed about its neatest explanation. Certain valleys, cliff paths and inlets contained more distance inside them than an ordinary map could show. Harper listened to this explanation from the excursion bus.

"So the road is bigger on the inside."

Elowen sighed. "That is offensively simple."

"Is it wrong?"

"Not enough for me to enjoy correcting you."

Ava immediately opened her notebook. Jasper looked out the window as the bus passed between two limestone ridges. For three seconds the sea disappeared behind rock. When it returned, the inlet beyond it was far wider than the coastline they had just entered should have allowed. No flash. No portal. Australia had simply made room. Lowtide itself looked more ordinary than Jasper expected.

There were cafés, small shops, houses, a medical clinic, a post office, a repair workshop and a bus stop. A delivery van waited outside a bakery. Phones worked. Digital payment terminals sat beside enchanted receipt printers. People wore normal clothes unless they had a reason not to. Harper stopped outside an electronics shop displaying waterproof spell-safe cases.

"I have been lied to. Magical towns have phone chargers."

"Why wouldn't they?" Ava asked.

"Because nobody puts them in paintings."

Their group spent the morning with staff guides, who explained how Lowtide worked without turning the excursion into a shopping trip. Magical trades existed beside ordinary ones. Some residents worked at Tidefall. Others travelled to ordinary Australian workplaces. Children attended different schools depending on their families and magical development. Deliveries came through controlled routes. Emergency services shared information with ordinary agencies when needed. Nothing about it felt like a secret kingdom. It felt like a town whose weirdest infrastructure happened to be invisible to most people. Jasper liked that.

At lunch they sat outside a bakery with sausage rolls and fruit. Harper had bought a heat-changing mug she absolutely did not need. Ava had purchased a small packet of archival pencils because apparently normal pencils became exciting if sold in a magical town. Jasper bought nothing for several minutes. Then he found a tiny brass model of an old Tidefall ferry in a second-hand shop window. It was missing one lifeboat. He stared at it. Harper looked between the model and Jasper. "You have to."

"I already have one."

"This one is also broken."

"Exactly."

Ava said, "Your purchasing logic is concerning." Jasper bought it. On the bus back, Lowtide disappeared more slowly than it had appeared. Buildings narrowed into the inlet, the inlet folded behind limestone, and eventually the road looked like an ordinary coastal road again. Jasper watched until the last roof vanished. For most of the year Tidefall had felt like the magical world. Lowtide corrected that. Tidefall was only one place inside it.
