The first-years finished exams on a Wednesday. By Thursday morning they had become impossible. There were still lessons, technically, but the serious part of the year had loosened. Teachers stopped assigning new written work. Foundations became games designed to test skills they already knew. Making and Mending turned into practical projects. Ocean Studies moved outside whenever weather allowed. At breakfast, Professor Elowen announced a supervised beach day for the following Monday. The first-year section erupted. Harper did not. Jasper noticed before she had time to hide it. The beach was not the tide-pool shelf where she had slipped harmlessly months earlier. This was a sandy cove south of Tidefall used for supervised swimming by older students and staff. First-years would have a shallow-water safety session, picnic and coastal games. Ava glanced at Jasper.

A completely ordinary school outing. Harper stared at her toast. Neither said anything. That was the first correct choice. After breakfast, Harper brought it up herself.

"I'm going."

They were halfway up the west stairs. Jasper said, "Okay." Ava said, "Okay." Harper stopped walking.

"That's it?"

"What do you want us to say?" Jasper asked.

"I don't know."

"Then okay."

Harper looked suspicious but continued. For the next four days, she talked about the beach constantly. Not fear. Equipment. What food they should bring. Whether sand counted as a useful magical material. Whether first-years would be allowed bodyboards. Whether Professor Elowen could legally stop her from building a sand fort large enough to require planning permission. Jasper knew what she was doing because he had done the same with the Sea mystery. Talk around the thing until the talking became easier than naming it. On Sunday night, the room was quiet. Ava had packed a towel, sunscreen, hat and spare shirt in a bag so organised it looked military. Jasper had packed almost the same things less impressively. Harper had packed snacks. Only snacks. Ava stared into her bag.

"Towel?"

"I'll get one."

"Hat?"

"I own one."

"Sunscreen?"

"School has some."

"Change of clothes?"

Harper looked toward her drawers. Ava sighed. Jasper stayed out of it until Harper said, very quietly, "What if I don't go in?" The room changed. Ava stopped packing. Jasper sat on the end of his bed.

"Then you don't."

Harper picked at the zip on her bag.

"Everyone will."

"Maybe."

"They'll think I'm scared."

Ava said, "You are scared." Harper glared. Ava did not back down.

"That is not an insult."

Harper looked away. Jasper remembered her standing under the arch of the repaired north ledge.

"You don't owe the beach proof either."

Harper looked at him.

"That's annoyingly like what Ward said to you."

"I know."

"I hate when lessons spread."

Ava smiled. Harper finally packed a towel. The next morning was hot enough that Tidefall seemed to glow. The first-years walked south under supervision wearing hats and carrying bags. The path stayed inland until the final turn, then dropped toward a wide sandy cove protected by rocky headlands. The sea was bright blue. No cliff below them. No deep drop. A shallow beach with small waves sliding onto sand. Harper stopped at the top of the final path. Jasper stopped one pace later. Ava stopped too. Mia nearly walked into all three.

"Why are we stopping?"

Harper said, "We're not." She continued down. No magic in water. No pushing. Ava noticed. She did not comment. Sand relay. Ava joined Mia. The beach session began with rules. Flags marked the swimming zone. First-years stayed no deeper than waist height unless directly with an instructor. No swimming outside the flags. If the whistle sounded twice, everyone returned to sand. Harper listened to every word. The first activity was not swimming. Teams carried buckets, built a wall and tested which shape resisted waves longest. Harper's group became absurdly competitive. Jasper worked with Soren and Leila. The waves destroyed every wall eventually. Professor Elowen said this was the lesson.

Harper said the lesson was that they needed more buckets. Children splashed. Gulls stole chips. Teachers complained about sunscreen. She stepped back. At lunch they sat under shade shelters eating sandwiches and fruit. The Titan-presence had been faint all morning. Jasper noticed it and let it remain background. This beach sat close enough to Tidefall for him to feel it, unlike home. But the presence did not dominate the water. The ocean was allowed to be ocean too. After lunch came shallow-water practice. Harper stood at the edge. Bare feet in wet sand. A small wave reached her ankles.

Nobody said anything. Another came. She stayed. He stood laughing. The ordinary stupidity helped. Stopped. A larger wave approached. Not large. Normal beach large. The fall. No ground. White water. Harper closed her eyes. Then opened them. She took another step. Waist depth. Good. Harper looked toward Jasper.

Jasper and Ava stood several metres away because crowding her would be its own kind of pressure. Mia ran past into the water, shouting because it was cold. Theo followed and immediately fell over in knee-deep water. Harper walked to shin depth. She turned sideways without thinking. The water hit her knees. For a second her face changed. Jasper saw the north ledge in it. Professor Elowen stood nearby but did not praise her.

"Come in."

He shook his head.

"Cold."

"Coward."

"Correct."

She splashed water toward him from several metres away. It did not reach.

"Get in."

Jasper walked reluctantly into the shallows. Ava followed because apparently she refused to be the only dry one. The three stood waist-deep. Harper's shoulders slowly lowered.

"This is fine."

Ava said, "Yes."

"Don't sound surprised."

"I wasn't."

A wave lifted them slightly. Jasper felt the Titan-presence touch his awareness. Not strong. A cool attention moving beneath ordinary surface noise. Harper saw his expression. Her smile vanished.

"Now?"

Jasper almost lied to protect the moment. Scared wasn't secret.

"Yeah. Faint."

Harper looked down at the water around her. The fear returned. Ava said quickly, "Presence does not mean action." Harper breathed.

"I know."

Another small wave passed. Nothing unusual. Harper remained. Jasper did not reach toward the presence. The presence did not intensify. After thirty seconds, Harper kicked water at him.

"Still coward."

He splashed back. Ava tried to escape and got caught in the crossfire. For two minutes all three became ordinary seven-year-olds in shallow water. That mattered. Later, Harper joined a supervised floating exercise. She did not like lying back with water under her ears. She did it for five seconds. Then ten. Then sat up.

"Done."

The instructor nodded. No cheer. No medal. Done. At the end of the day the first-years walked back sun-tired and sandy. Harper carried her shoes. Ava complained about sand inside hers. Jasper had a red patch on one shoulder despite sunscreen. Professor Elowen said his mother would be unimpressed. He knew. At the ridge above Tidefall, Harper stopped and looked back at the cove.

"I don't love the ocean."

Jasper said, "You don't have to."

"I like beaches though."

"Allowed."

"And pools."

Ava said, "You are creating categories." Harper grinned.

"Learning from the worst."

They returned to Room Fourteen. Harper put a tiny shell on Bag Bed. Ava stared.

"We cleaned this."

"New history."

"We are leaving soon."

"Exactly. Need final history."

The shell stayed. That night Harper slept without waking. So did Jasper. The Sea moved outside. Nothing needed to happen.
