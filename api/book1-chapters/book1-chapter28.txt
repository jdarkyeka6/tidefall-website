By breakfast, everyone knew Harper had fallen. By lunch, everyone knew the sea had caught her. By dinner, Harper had apparently fallen from the highest tower during a lightning storm and been lifted back to the academy by a twenty-metre wave. Harper had not even returned from the infirmary yet. Rumours were efficient. Mia sat beside Jasper at lunch.

"Someone in North residence says you made it happen."

Jasper stopped eating.

"Made what happen?"

"The wave."

Ava looked up sharply.

"On what evidence?"

Mia shrugged.

"Because you were there when the Ocean Studies weird thing happened too."

Jasper's stomach turned. There it was. The exact mistake. Response means control. He pushed his plate away.

"I didn't."

"I know."

"How?"

Mia looked confused.

"Because you looked more scared than anyone."

That was not evidence either. But Jasper appreciated it. The school made an announcement that afternoon. Not about magic. About safety. The north observation ledge closed while staff inspected the old barrier posts and drainage channel. All first-year coastal classes moved to the main terrace. Professor Crowe explained that winter water had loosened grit under part of the painted boundary and that the lower gap between two old posts should have been covered.

"The academy failed to account for a hazard," she said during first-year assembly. "We are correcting it."

Jasper noticed that wording. Not Harper failed. Not freak accident. The academy. Harper returned after dinner. The west residence greeted her like she had won a war. She hated it.

"Stop clapping."

Nobody stopped. Mia hugged her. Soren handed her a book because apparently that was how he handled emotional situations. Theo asked whether she remembered the fall. Mae told him not to ask. Harper said, "I remember enough." Jasper and Ava followed. Harper shut the door. Then escaped to Room Fourteen.

"I hate everyone."

Ava said, "You liked Mia."

"I hate almost everyone."

She sat on her bed. The west-stair pebble remained in her pocket. Jasper noticed but did not mention it.

"People think you made the wave," Harper said.

He looked down.

"Mia told me."

"They're idiots."

Ava said, "They are drawing a simple causal link from repeated correlation." Harper looked at her.

"Idiots."

"Simplified, yes."

Jasper sat at the desk.

"What if teachers think that too?"

Ava said, "Elowen doesn't."

"Thorne won't."

Harper's certainty helped. Then she added, "If he does, I'll tell him he's wrong."

"Please don't threaten the Headmaster."

"I didn't say threaten."

The next morning, Thorne held a meeting in his study. This time Professor Elowen and Crowe were there too. Harper's parents joined by a secure communication mirror in the wall, which was the first genuinely impressive long-distance magic Harper had seen. She spent thirty seconds being amazed before remembering the situation. Her mother looked like she had not slept. Her father looked worse. Thorne explained the physical investigation first. Loose grit. Drainage. Barrier gap. Repairs. Then the water.

"We have instrument records from the harbour," Crowe said. "The swell beneath the ledge was not consistent with wind or background tide."

Harper's mum closed her eyes briefly.

"So something magical happened."

"Yes."

Thorne did not hide behind maybe. Jasper appreciated that.

"Was my daughter targeted?"

Thorne's face tightened.

"We have no evidence of an attack."

Harper said, "It helped." Her parents looked at her.

"I don't like it," she added quickly. "But it did."

Jasper spoke before anyone asked him.

"I felt the presence before the wave moved. It wasn't reacting to me."

Harper's dad looked at him through the mirror.

"How do you know?"

Jasper thought carefully.

"Because I didn't do anything. It was already there."

Ava added, "The wave formed before Jasper reached the wall." Crowe nodded.

"Witness statements support that."

Thorne looked at Jasper.

"This matters."

Jasper knew.

"Communication is not command."

"Yes."

Harper's mum leaned toward the mirror.

"Harper, do you want to come home?"

Harper froze. Nobody had asked that yet.

"Now?"

"For a while. Or for the term. Whatever you need."

Harper looked around the study. At Jasper. Ava. Elowen. Then at her parents.

"No."

Her mum breathed out.

"Are you sure?"

"I want to stay."

"That can change."

"I know."

Her father said, "No hero points for staying if you want home." Harper smiled faintly.

"I know."

The meeting ended with more safety rules and no explanation of why the presence chose Harper. Outside, students stared as the trio crossed Founders' Courtyard. A younger first-year whispered, "That's her." Harper stopped. Jasper expected anger. Instead she turned.

"Hi."

The girl nearly jumped.

"Hi."

"I fell. I'm fine. The teachers fixed the ledge. That's all."

The girl nodded rapidly. Harper kept walking. Jasper caught up.

"That's not all."

"I know."

"Then why say it?"

Harper shoved her hands into her pockets.

"Because I don't want to be the girl the ocean saved."

Ava walked on her other side.

"You aren't."

Harper looked at her. Ava continued.

"You are Harper. That happened to you. It isn't your name."

Harper was quiet. Then said, "That was actually good." Ava looked pleased.

"Thank you."

"Don't get used to it."

Jasper smiled. The rumours lasted another week. Then someone in Year Two accidentally turned a practice dummy bright purple and school attention moved on. Children were efficient that way too.
