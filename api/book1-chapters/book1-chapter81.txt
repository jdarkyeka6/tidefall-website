The second message from the former Deepwater student was shorter than the first. It was not addressed to Jasper. That mattered. Thorne asked him to come to the Headmaster's study after lunch. Harper and Ava did not come this time, not because they had been excluded from something dangerous, but because another person's privacy belonged partly to that person. Jasper had learned the difference. Thorne held a folded sheet. "They received the summary of our revised safety guidance."

"Are they okay with it?"

"Very. They gave permission for me to share one sentence with you."

Jasper waited. Thorne read it aloud.

"Listening became dangerous when I started needing every answer to be for me."

Jasper sat back. The sentence was not dramatic enough to sound like ancient wisdom. That made it harder to dismiss.

"Did they know it was a Titan?" he asked.

"They believe so. They still do not claim to know everything they experienced."

"Do they still sense it?"

"Rarely."

Jasper looked toward the western window.

"Do they miss it?"

Thorne's expression changed slightly. "Sometimes." That answer surprised him most. The Water Titan had frightened Jasper, exhausted him and filled his year with questions. It had also become familiar. The thought of losing the sense completely produced a small hollow feeling he did not like examining.

"You can be careful without becoming afraid of yourself," Thorne said. "And you can listen without turning listening into the only thing you are."

Jasper nodded. When he left, he did not tell Harper and Ava the private parts of the conversation. He shared only the sentence Thorne had been allowed to share. Ava wrote it into the Chronicle. Harper underlined the word needing.

The second letter from the historical Deepwater communicator was shorter than the first. It was not meant for Jasper. That mattered. Thorne told him about it because the writer had given permission for one part to be shared. Jasper sat in the Headmaster's study after lunch. No Harper. No Ava. Not because they were excluded from a danger. Because this conversation belonged partly to another person's privacy. Jasper had learned the difference. Thorne held a folded sheet.

Jasper stared at him. That was not the sentence he expected. The first had been clear. If it answers you, do not mistake being heard for being obeyed. This one felt less like a rule. More like something learned late.

Annoying. Probably correct. Jasper looked toward the window. The Sea glittered beyond it. For months he had thought about Deepwater as warning. A child who heard something ancient. Tested it. Mistook response for control. Someone got hurt. Rules changed. That story had become the shadow behind every choice Jasper made. Don't test. Don't reach alone. Don't assume. Don't control. Useful lessons. But a life made entirely of avoiding someone else's mistake would still be shaped by that mistake.

That might have been the most useful Deepwater fact Jasper ever learned. The story had always frozen its communicator at the worst moment. A mistake near a dock. An injury. Shame. But the child had grown up. Lived decades afterward. Water had not consumed their entire identity. Neither had the mistake.

Ava sighed. The sentence followed Jasper into the final week. Not a warning. Permission. He could be careful without becoming afraid of himself. He could listen without making listening the only thing he was. He could enter Choosing Tide without treating every movement of water as a test he might fail. Deepwater belonged in the school's memory. It did not need to own his future. Deepwater belonged in Tidefall's memory. It did not need to own Jasper's future.
