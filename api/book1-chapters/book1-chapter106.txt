The ceremony did not end when Jasper reached Brannor. That was easy to forget because Room Fourteen briefly behaved as though the academy had won a championship. There were still formal welcomes. Family photographs. Scarf edging. A short Order introduction. Callum, who had stopped his first attempt, received extra applause when Nocten called his name among its new members. Thorne thanked the families. The basin gates closed. Water drained slowly back toward the sea. Jasper kept expecting someone to ask what happened during his placement. Nobody did for six minutes. Then everyone did. His mother reached him first.

"Are you all right?"

"Yeah."

"It stopped."

"Yeah."

"Was that..."

She looked toward the basin. Jasper nodded.

"Water noticed."

His father's eyebrows rose.

"And Brannor?"

"I don't think that part was Water."

His mother studied him.

"You don't think."

"I don't know."

She smiled.

"Good answer."

Harper's family arrived in a cluster. Her mother hugged Jasper because apparently Choosing Tide had erased personal-space law. Ava's father shook his hand and then seemed to decide that was insufficient and hugged him too. Ava looked horrified by all adults. Harper looked delighted. Mia's family joined. Soon Brannor's new first-years stood in a mess of relatives, towels and grey scarves with temporary trim. The Order representative tried to organize a photograph. This failed repeatedly. Harper blinked in one. Theo, from Velmor, appeared behind them in another despite not belonging to Brannor. A small child who was nobody's student wandered into the third. The fourth photo was acceptable. Harper preferred the third. After photographs, families were invited to a Brannor welcome lunch. Jasper noticed the threshold. Same stone arch. Different feeling.

The new students entered the Brannor wing properly for the first time as members. Not visitors from Open Week. A temporary banner hung across the common hall. WELCOME, NEW BRANNOR. Under it someone had added in smaller writing: YES, YOU STILL HAVE HOMEWORK NEXT YEAR. Harper stopped.

"Cruel."

Priya Nand, the older student from Open Week, stood beside the banner.

"I thought of you."

"I feel targeted."

"Correct."

New Brannor students were shown their likely Year Two residence area. Not exact rooms yet. Those depended on final numbers and practical arrangements. Harper immediately asked whether Jasper and Ava could stay in the same room. Priya looked at the three.

"Possibly. Requests are considered, but rooms are not friendship prizes."

Harper opened her mouth. Ava stepped on her shoe.

"Ow."

Jasper laughed. The possibility was enough. They would all be in the same wing. Same common spaces. Same Order mentors. Even if Room Fourteen did not continue exactly, the geography of their lives would remain close. At lunch Brannor's head of Order, Professor Darius Keene, spoke for five minutes. Jasper had seen him around school but never had a class with him. Keene was broad-shouldered, greying and looked more like someone who repaired boats than someone who gave speeches. Perhaps because he did repair boats.

"Brannor is often translated badly," he said.

Harper sat forward.

"People call us brave. We value courage. People call us strong. We value strength. But neither word means much without purpose."

Behind him the Order motto had been carved into a stone lintel. STRENGTH EXISTS TO PROTECT. Keene tapped the table.

"Protection is not possession. It is not deciding other people's choices because you are afraid for them. It is not proving you can endure more pain than everybody else. It is not volunteering for every dangerous thing in the hope someone notices."

Jasper felt Harper go very still beside him. Then realized the speech had hit him too. Protection is not possession. His overprotective phase after Harper's fall. Brannor had chosen all three. Maybe all three needed different versions of the same lesson. Keene continued.

"Sometimes Brannor moves first. Sometimes Brannor holds a line. Sometimes Brannor steps back so someone else can lead. You will spend years learning which moment is which."

Ava wrote it down. Harper whispered, "Of course you did." After lunch, Priya took the new students to Brannor's small courtyard. Every newly placed student planted nothing. Jasper had expected a tree or symbolic flower. Instead, each received a plain stone.

"Write one thing you think Brannor means," Priya said.

They used chalk. Harper wrote ACTION. Then stared at it. Rubbed it off. Wrote CHOOSE WHEN TO ACT. Ava wrote PROTECT WHAT MATTERS WITHOUT NEEDING CERTAINTY. It barely fit. Jasper wrote LISTEN BEFORE HELPING. Mia wrote STAY. The stones went into a temporary circle. At the end of each year, Priya explained, students could change the words.

"Most do."

Harper looked at Jasper's.

"That's very you."

"Yours is very long."

"Ava's is longer."

"Everything I write is appropriately detailed."

The day ended with families leaving the Order wing. Jasper hugged his parents goodbye. They would return in two days for departure. His mother touched the new edge of his scarf.

"Brannor."

"Yeah."

"Happy?"

Jasper looked at Harper arguing with Priya about whether carrying five chairs at once counted as efficient. Ava stood beside the motto, rereading it.

"Yeah."

Very. That night Room Fourteen had three Brannor-edged scarves hanging from three beds. Harper stood in the middle of the room.

"This looks correct."

Ava said, "Confirmation bias."

"Happiness."

"Both can coexist."

Jasper sat on the Bag Bed. The history box had returned. For the first time, they knew where it would go next. Brannor. Maybe a new room. Maybe different rooms and a common shelf. But Brannor. Harper dropped onto the bed beside him.

"We worried for nothing."

Ava shook her head.

"No."

Harper looked at her.

"We worried because the outcome mattered and was uncertain. We learned we would remain friends before the outcome became convenient. That wasn't nothing."

Harper stared.

"Could you say one normal sentence per day?"

Ava threw a pillow. Harper caught it. Jasper laughed. Outside, Water remained quiet. The Sea had already had its part in the day. It did not need to explain itself afterward.
