The storm did not end because Jasper communicated with the Sea. This was important. Rain still hammered Tidefall. Wind still screamed around towers. The natural front continued moving across the coast because weather did not care that one magical problem had been solved. The internal shelter remained occupied for another six hours. That made the aftermath feel less heroic and more like being tired on a hard floor. Harper complained about the blankets. Ava fell asleep sitting against the wall with her notebook on her lap. Jasper ate two emergency crackers and decided they tasted like cardboard with salt. Theo asked whether the giant surge meant the Sea was officially on their side. Jasper said, "No."

Theo looked disappointed.

"It helped."

"It wanted the gate open."

"Same result."

"Different reason."

Theo thought about it.

"Okay."

Mia, sitting nearby, said, "If someone saves you because they also wanted something, does it count?" Harper answered before Jasper.

"Ask me later."

Nobody laughed immediately. Then Harper smiled. The tension broke. At dawn, the red weather flag remained. Students were moved from internal halls back to residence in supervised groups once engineers cleared the route. The academy looked battered but standing. Water had seeped under some west doors. Branches filled inner courtyards. One covered walkway lost several roof tiles. No major injuries. No collapsed towers. Room Fourteen's window had held. Harper touched the frame.

"Knew it."

Ava said, "You predicted breakage."

"I evolved."

Bag Bed had shifted during the night because someone left the storage chest unlocked. The historical collection lay across the floor. Harper picked up the paper boat.

"Survived."

"Unlike the real barge," Jasper said.

Ava looked toward the west. The work barge had been carried out beyond the breakwater. Staff later found it grounded on a sand shelf south of the academy, damaged but recoverable. The storm eased by late morning. At noon the flag changed from red to yellow. At two, students were allowed into Founders' Courtyard in small groups. Jasper, Harper and Ava went together. The sea looked wild. Whitecaps covered the surface. The repaired breakwater had lost one temporary beam but remained intact. From high above, the lower harbour entrance was visible. The gate stood fully open. Debris had been thrown onto the outer rocks. Jasper felt the Titan-presence far offshore. Not focused on Tidefall. He did not reach.

The storm still moved through it. Harper leaned against the inner wall.

"It listened to you."

Jasper's skin prickled at the word.

"I think."

Ava said, "It changed timing after your request."

"That doesn't mean I caused it."

"No."

"Could have been coincidence."

Ava looked at him.

"You are allowed to believe evidence too."

Jasper frowned. She continued.

"Not certainty. Evidence. You reached. The pressure held. Staff cleared. You showed the empty bridge. The surge followed. We have instrument times."

Harper said, "It listened." Being heard. Not being obeyed. Jasper looked at the water.

"Okay."

The word felt dangerous and true.

"It listened."

Harper smiled. Jasper added, "It also nearly destroyed the harbour because it wanted the gate open."

"Less cute."

"Exactly."

Professor Crowe joined them. She had not slept either.

"Harbour report."

Ava immediately took out her notebook. Crowe sighed but continued.

"Gate mechanism damaged but repairable. Debris cleared. Inner platform lost two rail sections. Work barge found. Nobody injured."

Harper said, "Because staff got out."

"Yes."

Crowe looked at Jasper.

"Your warning contributed to the timing of that evacuation."

Jasper felt uncomfortable. Crowe noticed.

"Contributed. Not caused. The harbour team already had storm procedure. Ava's interval estimate accelerated the final clearance. Harper helped keep the internal hall calm enough that Elowen could remain with you. Many things mattered."

Harper blinked.

"I carried water."

"You also stopped three students trying to leave the hall."

"Theo."

"Theo was two of them."

Harper laughed. Crowe's expression softened.

"Emergencies are rarely one heroic action. They are systems working or failing together. Last night enough worked."

Ava looked pleased by the word systems. Jasper looked at the gate. Deepwater had been a system failing around one child's certainty. Last night had been different. Adults listened without surrendering responsibility. Ava treated patterns as information, not commands. Harper acted without running into the danger herself. Jasper communicated without believing the Sea belonged to him. And the Sea made its own choice. Maybe that was what growing around a mystery looked like. The storm clouds moved east. Sunlight broke through over the water. For one moment the sea flashed silver. Jasper felt a brief touch of attention. No words. No image. Then gone. He smiled. Harper caught him.

"What?"

"Nothing."

Ava groaned.

"Stop saying nothing."

Jasper laughed.

"Sea. Brief. That's all."

Ava nodded. Harper looked at the horizon.

"Good."

This time, Jasper agreed.
