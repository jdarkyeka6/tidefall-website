Harper refused to visit the north observation ledge for three weeks. Nobody asked her to. The ledge remained closed for repairs anyway. Then one morning a notice appeared in the first-year hall: NORTH OBSERVATION LEDGE REOPENED NEW BARRIER INSTALLED STAFF-SUPERVISED LESSONS RESUME NEXT WEEK Harper read it twice. Then walked away. At lunch she did not mention it. During handball she hit the ball hard enough to bounce it over Mia's head and lose the point. At dinner she asked whether next week's Ocean Studies was indoor. Ava checked the timetable.

"North ledge."

Harper nodded. Jasper waited. Nothing. That night, Harper was still awake after lights-out. Jasper could tell because she kept turning over. Finally she whispered, "I don't want to go." Ava answered first.

"Okay."

Harper was quiet.

"That's it?"

"Yes."

"You're not going to say I should?"

"No."

Jasper said, "You don't have to." Harper sighed.

"But I want to not care."

That was different. Jasper understood immediately.

"You care."

"I know."

"That's allowed."

"I know."

Her voice sounded annoyed with the entire concept. Ava said, "You could ask to see it before class. With a teacher. No crowd." Harper stopped moving.

"Why?"

"Because the lesson adds pressure."

"And seeing it alone doesn't?"

"Not alone. With us and staff."

Harper thought.

"Maybe."

The next day she asked Professor Elowen. Elowen said yes without making it a big thing. Harper stopped before crossing. The repairs were obvious. Everything looked safer. Professor Elowen waited. Harper took one step. Then another. Stopped. The sea rolled below. No strange swell.

Saturday morning, the four of them walked to the north ledge. Professor Elowen unlocked the final gate. The old stone barrier had been raised and reinforced with vertical metal rails. The drainage groove had been filled and resurfaced. The painted student line sat farther back. It also looked exactly like the place Harper had fallen. She stood under the arch. Jasper did not say you can do it. Ava did not explain risk percentages. She reached the inner line. No pressure in Jasper's chest. Harper looked at the outer barrier.

"I went through there."

Professor Elowen nodded.

"Between the old posts."

"Yeah."

Harper moved closer, still behind the new painted line. Her breathing changed. Jasper noticed but pretended not to. She stood another minute. Then turned around.

"Done."

Elowen nodded. They left. On the stairs down to breakfast, Harper's shoulders loosened.

"That was awful."

Ava said, "You did it." Harper looked at her.

"Do not make this inspirational."

"I wasn't."

"You had the voice."

Jasper laughed. The first Ocean Studies lesson back on the ledge was still difficult. Harper stood farther from the line than everyone else. Nobody commented. Halfway through, a gust of wind moved loose paper near the wall. Harper flinched. Jasper saw. She saw him see.

"Don't."

"Wasn't."

They continued the lesson. At the end, Harper walked out under her own power. No dramatic victory. No fear magically gone. She still hated the ledge. She came back next week anyway. That was enough.
