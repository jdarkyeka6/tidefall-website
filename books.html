ABOUT THE BOOK

Blurb

Tidefall Academy stands where stone meets an ocean that does not always behave like an ocean should. For its students, learning magic is only the beginning. The deeper they go into Tidefall, the clearer it becomes that the sea is watching, the Academy is hiding things, and some currents were never meant to be followed quietly.

Chapter One

THE WATER WITHOUT A SKY

Jasper Holloway was underwater, but he could breathe.

That was the first thing that was wrong.

The second was the silence.

There were no gulls. No wind. No fizz of foam collapsing against sand. No distant cars from the road behind his house, no neighbour dragging bins across concrete, no television murmuring from downstairs. Even the water around him made no sound. It held him upright in a blue-black place without a surface or a floor, his bare feet hanging over a darkness that seemed to continue forever.

Jasper looked up.

There should have been sunlight somewhere above him.

Instead there was only a pale silver haze, far away and somehow no farther than the end of his fingers at the same time.

He lifted one hand.

The water slid between his fingers, warm where it should have been cold. Tiny threads of colour moved through it. Not bright enough to illuminate the dark, only enough to show that he was not sinking.

He tried to swim upward.

His arms moved. His legs kicked. The silver haze did not come any closer.

Jasper stopped.

The water was not pulling him down.

That would have made sense. Water pulled. People sank. You kicked harder and tried not to swallow anything.

This water simply refused to let distance behave properly.

Something shifted below.

Jasper went still.

It was not a fish.

He knew that without seeing it. A fish was a quick thing, even a big one. It flicked or turned or vanished. Whatever moved beneath him was slower than that. The darkness itself seemed to change shape, as though some enormous part of it had decided to face another direction.

A pressure passed through his chest.

Not pain.

Not a voice.

Attention.

Jasper's heart began to race.

The water around him changed with it.

A pulse moved out from his body.

He felt it rather than saw it, a soft pressure travelling in a widening circle through the impossible dark.

Then something answered from below.

One pulse.

Slow.

Deep.

Not his.

Jasper forgot to breathe even though breathing was somehow still possible.

He stared into the black.

For a moment there was nothing.

Then he thought he saw a shape.

Not clearly. A curve where there should have been empty water. A rise like the slope of a hill, except hills did not move and this one had just turned beneath him. It might have been closer than he thought. It might have been kilometres down.

The water pressed gently against his ribs.

Jasper opened his mouth.

“What are you?”

The words did not bubble away. They did not sound either. They simply existed for a second and vanished into the depth.

The presence below shifted.

Something flashed across Jasper's mind.

Not a picture exactly. More like remembering something that had never happened to him.

Moonlight breaking across enormous waves.

A black coastline viewed from far out at sea.

Stone under impossible pressure.

The feeling of waiting so long that years stopped being useful.

Then it was gone.

Jasper's fingers tingled.

The warm water tightened around his hand for half a second.

He pulled back.

The silver haze above him vanished.

Darkness closed in.

The huge thing beneath him turned once more.

Jasper woke.

He sat upright so quickly that his blanket slid onto the floor.

His room was bright with early summer sunlight.

For several seconds he did not know where he was.

His heart hammered. His pyjama shirt clung to his back. His hand was raised in front of him as though he expected water to be wrapped around it.

Nothing was there.

His bedroom was exactly as it had been when he went to sleep.

The shelf under the window was crowded with old books and a model ferry missing one tiny red lifeboat. A framed drawing from when he was five hung crookedly beside the wardrobe. His suitcase stood open near the door, packed in neat rows because his mum had shown him three times how to fit more clothes by folding instead of stuffing.

A navy jumper had been laid over the chair.

On the chest was a small silver crest he had still not got used to seeing.

TIDEFALL ACADEMY.

Jasper put one hand against his chest.

His heartbeat slowed.

Outside, somewhere beyond the roofs, came the faintest sound of the real ocean.

He crossed to the window.

Their house sat far enough inland that the sea was only visible through a gap between buildings at the end of the street. This morning it was a narrow strip of blue under a white-hot sky. Sunlight glittered across it.

A wave broke.

Another followed.

Nothing stared back.

Probably.

“Jasper!” his mum called from downstairs. “Breakfast!”

He looked at the suitcase.

Today was the day.

Seven years old, one suitcase, one school he had never seen, and one boat ride his parents had spent the last month pretending was completely normal.

Jasper looked at the water once more.

The dream had been a dream.

That was what dreams were. They felt real until you woke up.

He knew that.

He told himself anyway.

The strip of ocean between the roofs flashed silver.

For half a second Jasper thought the whole surface went perfectly still.

Then a bus passed the end of the street and blocked the view.

When it was gone, the sea was moving again.

Jasper backed away from the window.

He did not mention the dream at breakfast.
